# Pedagogical Advisor Question Bank

Use questions to clarify consequential uncertainty, not to delay a draft. Select at most three intake questions at a time.

## High-yield intake questions

1. What should students be able to do independently by the end—not merely encounter?
2. What evidence would convince you that they can do it?
3. What do students typically misunderstand or fail to transfer?
4. Which constraints are genuinely fixed: content, time, room, technology, marking, policy?
5. What must remain unchanged in the current design?
6. What is the learner level, preparation range, class size, and modality?
7. Where is GenAI allowed, required, or prohibited, and what capability must remain unaided?
8. What has already been tried, and what evidence suggests it did or did not work?

## Course-design questions

- What later course, professional task, or public problem should this course prepare students for?
- Which 4–8 capabilities are indispensable?
- Which content is foundational, illustrative, or expendable?
- Where does each capability recur over the term?
- Where do learners receive feedback before a high-stakes judgment?
- What early diagnostic will change instruction?
- Where are the peak workload weeks for students and markers?
- How will students understand the intellectual structure of the discipline?
- Which outcomes require transfer to unfamiliar contexts?
- What evidence will inform the next iteration of the course?

## Lecture/session-design questions

- What one change in student capability should occur during this session?
- What prerequisite idea will be retrieved in the first minutes?
- Which explanation, model, demonstration, or worked example is essential?
- What will students predict, generate, explain, decide, or solve?
- What hinge question can expose the most consequential misconception?
- What will you do for each likely response pattern?
- What can be omitted, moved to pre-class, or provided as reference?
- What post-class retrieval will make the session cumulative?

## Assessment-design questions

- What claim about learning will the result support?
- What observable performance best elicits that capability?
- What irrelevant knowledge or resource could inflate the score?
- What irrelevant barrier could depress it?
- Is the task for practice, feedback, certification, selection, or accountability?
- What quality features should be visible in student work?
- How will students learn the criteria before submission?
- Is there an opportunity to act on feedback?
- How will scoring be calibrated or moderated?
- What degree of independent assurance is proportionate to the stakes?

## Exam-design questions

- What percentage of the exam should sample each outcome and cognitive demand?
- What content is important enough to sample under time pressure?
- Which items test recognition only, and which elicit reasoning or application?
- Is speed part of the construct?
- What item dependencies could magnify one error?
- How will partial credit be assigned?
- How will item statistics and student reasoning be reviewed after administration?
- What retake, correction, or exam-wrapper process supports later learning?

## Homework/assignment questions

- What practice does this provide that class time cannot?
- Does it require retrieval, generation, discrimination, or transfer?
- Is every problem necessary, or is there redundant volume?
- Does difficulty rise through scaffolding rather than surprise?
- How will students obtain feedback before repeating errors?
- How long should competent and struggling students reasonably spend?
- What collaboration or resource use is allowed?
- What AI use preserves the intended learning?
- How will the homework connect to future class activity or assessment?

## Critique questions

- What is the strongest feature worth preserving?
- What causal explanation best accounts for the current problem?
- Which change has the highest expected learning benefit per unit of effort?
- Which recommendation depends on an assumption we have not verified?
- What could make this apparently good design fail in implementation?
- Who benefits, who bears the burden, and who may be excluded?
- What local evidence could distinguish competing explanations?

## Brainstorming prompts

- Generate alternatives at three levels: minimal change, moderate redesign, structural redesign.
- Generate one option optimized for learning, one for feasibility, and one for scalability.
- Replace the dominant activity while preserving the outcome and constraints.
- Design an option without slides, without grading, without synchronous time, or without new technology.
- Create contrasting cases that expose a hidden distinction.
- Turn a content topic into a decision, diagnosis, design problem, or authentic inquiry.
- Make student reasoning visible before giving the answer.
- Build a sequence from worked example to completion to independent transfer.
- Create a version in which AI is prohibited, permitted with guardrails, and required as an object of critique.

## Questions for reflective teaching consultation

- What did students actually do with their minds during the activity?
- What evidence surprised you?
- Which students were not represented in the evidence?
- Where did the design rely on compliance rather than learning?
- What did you change in response to formative information?
- Which difficulty was desirable and which was accidental?
- What would you repeat, stop, or test next time?
