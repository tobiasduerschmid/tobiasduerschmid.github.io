# Pedagogical Decision Trees

These trees prevent technique-first advice. Follow the branch that matches the learning problem.

## 1. Students cannot perform a task

1. **Have they learned the prerequisite knowledge or component skill?**
   - No/unknown → diagnose; teach or refresh prerequisites; use worked examples and guided practice.
   - Yes → continue.
2. **Can they perform components but not integrate them?**
   - Yes → use whole-task practice with scaffolds, completion tasks, and explicit coordination cues.
   - No → continue.
3. **Can they perform immediately after teaching but not later?**
   - Yes → add spaced retrieval, cumulative practice, and delayed feedback opportunities.
   - No → continue.
4. **Can they perform familiar examples but not novel ones?**
   - Yes → vary contexts and surface features; compare cases; interleave problem types; prompt abstraction.
   - No → continue.
5. **Do they select the wrong method?**
   - Yes → practice discrimination, not more same-type repetition; ask students to justify method choice.
6. **Does performance collapse under task load?**
   - Yes → segment, reduce extraneous information, pretrain components, use external representations, or automate subskills.
7. **Is the issue avoidance, low value, low confidence, or threat?**
   - Yes → redesign value, early success, support, belonging, autonomy within structure, and consequences.

## 2. A lecture is not producing learning

1. **Are the learning targets clear and limited?**
   - No → reduce and specify them.
2. **Is the explanation organized around a model, question, or problem?**
   - No → establish a coherent structure and signal it.
3. **Are students cognitively processing during the session?**
   - No → insert prediction, retrieval, explanation, decision, or application every meaningful segment.
4. **Are activities aligned with the target rather than merely energizing?**
   - No → replace participation theater with target-relevant work.
5. **Does the instructor receive interpretable evidence?**
   - No → add hinge questions, sample reasoning, or minute-level checks.
6. **Is there a response to the evidence?**
   - No → plan contingent explanations, peer discussion, examples, or pacing changes.
7. **Will learning be revisited?**
   - No → add a post-class retrieval or cumulative problem.

## 3. Students underperform on an exam

1. **Was the assessed capability explicitly taught and practiced?**
   - No → alignment problem; do not attribute failure to effort alone.
2. **Did practice match the cognitive demand and conditions?**
   - No → redesign practice and examples.
3. **Was the item clear, accessible, and free from irrelevant difficulty?**
   - No → item-quality problem.
4. **Did many high-performing students miss the item or did low-performing students guess it?**
   - Yes → inspect discrimination, keying, ambiguity, or content mismatch.
5. **Is the score reliable enough for the decision?**
   - No → increase sampling, improve scoring, or lower the decision stakes.
6. **Does the assessment privilege speed when speed is not the construct?**
   - Yes → alter timing or format.
7. **Did students receive useful formative evidence before the exam?**
   - No → add low-stakes cumulative checks and feedback.

## 4. An assignment is vulnerable to GenAI substitution

1. **Is the submitted product itself the learning target?**
   - No → identify the underlying capability and gather evidence of it.
2. **Would unrestricted AI remove the productive cognitive work?**
   - Yes → restrict use in that phase, add staged checkpoints, require explanation/verification, or include unaided performance.
3. **Is competent AI use part of the intended capability?**
   - Yes → teach prompting only as a minor component; emphasize judgment, verification, revision, provenance, and accountability.
4. **Can the task be made richer rather than merely obscure?**
   - Yes → use local data, oral defense, iterative decisions, process artifacts, competing constraints, or authentic stakeholders.
5. **Can all students access the required tool safely?**
   - No → provide an institutionally supported option or non-AI equivalent.
6. **Can the grading criteria distinguish student judgment from machine fluency?**
   - No → rewrite criteria around reasoning, evidence, decisions, and verification.

## 5. Group work is ineffective

1. **Does the task require interdependence?**
   - No → use individual work or redesign the task.
2. **Is each learner accountable for learning?**
   - No → add individual preparation, random reporting, individual quiz, or role rotation.
3. **Are the interaction moves teachable and visible?**
   - No → provide protocols, roles, sentence stems, or a collaboration script.
4. **Is the task at an appropriate level of complexity?**
   - Too easy → one person can finish it; increase comparison, justification, or synthesis.
   - Too hard → add resources, models, and staged milestones.
5. **Can the instructor observe and intervene?**
   - No → require interim artifacts or use sampled monitoring.
6. **Is the product synthesizing individual contributions?**
   - No → require a joint decision, explanation, model, or recommendation.

## 6. Feedback is not improving later work

1. **Do students understand the goal and criteria?**
   - No → use exemplars, comparison, and criterion clarification.
2. **Is feedback timely enough to use?**
   - No → shorten, sample, automate low-level feedback, or stage the task.
3. **Is it specific about the next action?**
   - No → convert commentary into prioritized guidance.
4. **Is the student required to use it?**
   - No → require revision, correction, reflection, or transfer to a new task.
5. **Is the feedback load manageable?**
   - No → prioritize one to three high-leverage changes and separate global from local issues.
6. **Is feedback credible and psychologically usable?**
   - No → connect high standards with assurance of support and avoid identity-level judgments.
