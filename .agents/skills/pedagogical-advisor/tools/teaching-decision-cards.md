# Teaching decision cards

Use these original operational translations when the diagnosis fits. The evidence applies to mechanisms and conditions, not every detail or time estimate in a recipe. Source IDs E001–E060 refer to `references/evidence-matrix.csv`; inspect linked primary/book evidence for consequential claims. Recent AI recipes use the 2026 evidence ledgers.

| Symptom and competing explanation | Concrete teaching move | Feedback and next evidence | Boundary, burden, and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students recognize terminology but cannot recall it; may have only reread | Close notes: “Write the three conditions for applying this rule, then make one example.” Reopen and correct in another color. | Revisit one condition and a new example next week. | Start with feedback and low stakes; inaccessible recall format can confound learning. 4–6 min estimate. Retrieval/spacing: E008–E010. |
| Students cannot start; may lack a procedure rather than motivation | Model one example with purpose labels; give a second with one missing decision. Ask why that step fits. | Reveal and explain the missing step; fade one more step on a new task. | Do not withhold needed content indefinitely. Supports may become redundant for experts. E013–E014; CS-F01 in `references/computing-foundations.md`. |
| Students execute one method everywhere; may not recognize conditions of use | Show three superficially similar cases requiring different strategies; ask for selection and one diagnostic feature before solving. | Contrast cases, then use a new context. | Interleaving can overwhelm before basic acquisition; use a small discriminable set. E011/E060. |
| Correct peer votes but weak individual understanding | Get an individual answer plus reason, discuss disagreements, revote, then answer a new isomorphic question independently. | Synthesize reasoning and revisit later. | Revote gain is immediate group-supported performance, not retention. 8–12 min estimate. E006/E027. |
| Misconception survives explanation | Elicit a prediction; demonstrate a counterexample; ask students to reconcile evidence with their original model. | Require a revised explanation and a boundary case. | Surprise alone does not cause conceptual change; explicitly teach the replacement model. E001; project sources 1/11/15. |
| Worked solution looks clear but students cannot transfer | Compare two examples: “What changes on the surface? What relationship stays invariant?” | Solve a third example without the labels; explain applicability. | Arbitrary context variation adds load; preserve the conceptual relationship. E002/E060. |
| AI-written code works but students cannot debug | Ask for an output prediction, a suspected failure, and a discriminating test before AI suggestions. | Inspect actual trace; revise hypothesis; solve a related fault without the helper. | Tool fluency and debugging are different targets; restore prerequisite tracing if needed. `knowledge/24_computing-education-and-ai.md`. |
| Tutor keeps asking questions and student stalls | Give the smallest missing explanation or a worked step, then ask for the next step with a reason. | Check progress after the next attempt; widen help if still blocked. | “Socratic” does not mean never explain. Guardrails are design-specific, not guaranteed learning gains. `references/ai-learning-evidence-2026.md`. |
| Feedback is ignored; may be too late, unclear, or unactionable | Return two criterion-linked comments before final submission; ask for a short revision plus one rejected-feedback justification. | Check whether the next version resolves the cited discrepancy. | Requiring a long reflection adds work without necessarily improving use. E025–E028. |
| Group product hides unequal participation | Each learner drafts a decision; group must compare conflicting reasons; each submits a new individual application. | Use the differences to diagnose who needs help. | Rotating roles alone cannot fix a divisible task or unsafe climate. E007/E042. |
| Low participation; may reflect preparation, threat, language, access | Allow silent thinking/writing first and several response channels; solicit reasons without ranking volunteers. | Sample all responses, address patterns, invite revision. | Do not equate public speed or confidence with mastery. E039–E040; source 10/13. |
| Course is overloaded; learning targets compete | Sort content into essential independent capability, tool-supported performance, reference knowledge, and optional enrichment. Remove or shorten one low-value task per addition. | Pilot novice completion time and inspect transfer evidence. | Categorization is a local judgment, not a universal taxonomy of expendable knowledge. E045–E046. |
| Students feel worse during harder learning | Explain the target and reason for the effort; compare confidence predictions with independent performance. | Discuss mismatch without dismissing student distress or workload. | Desirable difficulty requires attainable success and support. E004/E020. |
| A slide is visually polished but teaches little | Turn the title into a precise claim; label the evidence in the figure; add a prediction or comparison students must resolve. | Have a learner explain the relationship without reading the title. | Do not impose decorative media or arbitrary word-count limits. E015–E019; `procedures/16_material-review-and-repair.md`. |
| AI activity is engaging but learning is unknown | Define one relevant independent measure before adoption; record assistance and collect a delayed variant where retention is claimed. | Keep, revise, or remove the ingredient based on learning plus burden. | One class pre/post comparison does not isolate causality; confidence/logs are insufficient. `procedures/18_assessment-and-feedback-clinic.md`. |

For any card, adapt format for accessibility without changing the capability assessed. Time estimates are illustrative. Do not cite this card itself as empirical proof.
