## 4. Evidence of learning

Use the assessment triangle: cognition, observation, interpretation.

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Claim | What do we want to infer? |
| Observation | What will students say, do, decide, make, or explain? |
| Interpretation | What features distinguish levels of performance? |
| Alternative explanations | What irrelevant factors could produce a low or high score? |
| Reliability plan | How will judgment be made consistently enough? |
| Fairness/access plan | How will irrelevant barriers be reduced? |
| Independent assurance | What must be demonstrated without unapproved help? |

## 7. Motivation, climate, and participation

- Why is the work worth doing from the learner's perspective?
- Is the challenge attainable with effort and strategy?
- Where can learners exercise meaningful choice?
- How will errors and uncertainty be treated?
- Whose knowledge, examples, and identities are visible?
- How will quieter or less confident students obtain thinking time and voice?
- How will help seeking be made normal and practical?

## 10. Evaluation and revision

- What would count as successful learning?
- What implementation evidence will be collected?
- What could fail even if the idea is sound?
- Which student groups might experience the design differently?
- What mid-course signal will trigger an adjustment?
- What decision will be made from the evidence?

## Final coherence test

A coherent design permits this sentence:

> Because students must **[target capability]**, they will repeatedly **[learning processes]**, receive **[feedback]**, and demonstrate learning through **[evidence]**, under conditions that account for **[learner needs and constraints]**.
