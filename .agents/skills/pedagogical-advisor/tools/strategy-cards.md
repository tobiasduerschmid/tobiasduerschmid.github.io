# Evidence-Informed Strategy Cards

Each card states a purpose, evidence status, implementation pattern, and common failure. Evidence labels follow `config/evidence-levels.yaml`.

## 1. Prior-knowledge diagnostic — A

**Use for:** identifying prerequisites, misconceptions, and variation before instruction.\
**Pattern:** 3–8 low-stakes items or a concept task; sample reasoning, not confidence alone; respond with bridges, grouping, or targeted explanation.\
**Failure:** collecting data but teaching the original plan unchanged.

## 2. Retrieval practice — A

**Use for:** durable access to foundational knowledge and concepts.\
**Pattern:** frequent, low-stakes, cumulative recall with corrective feedback; include free recall, short explanation, or application.\
**Failure:** turning every retrieval into punitive grading or using recognition-only questions exclusively.

## 3. Spaced practice — A

**Use for:** reducing forgetting and supporting long-term retention.\
**Pattern:** revisit important learning after increasing delays; roll earlier material into quizzes, examples, and assignments.\
**Failure:** “review week” as the only return to earlier material.

## 4. Interleaving and variation — B

**Use for:** learning to discriminate problem types and select strategies.\
**Pattern:** mix related categories after initial understanding; require method identification and comparison.\
**Failure:** random mixing before learners possess the component methods.

## 5. Worked examples — A/B

**Use for:** novice learning of complex procedures and schemas.\
**Pattern:** integrate steps with explanations; prompt self-explanation; compare examples; transition to completion tasks and independent problems.\
**Failure:** leaving examples in place after they become redundant or presenting unexplained solution dumps.

## 6. Faded scaffolding — A/B

**Use for:** moving from supported to independent performance.\
**Pattern:** model → partially completed task → prompts/hints → independent task; fade based on evidence, not calendar alone.\
**Failure:** removing support abruptly or retaining it so long that learners never choose strategies.

## 7. Prediction before explanation — B

**Use for:** activating models, surfacing misconceptions, and preparing attention.\
**Pattern:** pose a consequential prediction; require commitment and rationale; reveal evidence; reconcile discrepancy.\
**Failure:** asking for a guess with no later comparison or explanation.

## 8. Peer instruction — A/B

**Use for:** conceptual questions with plausible misconceptions.\
**Pattern:** individual vote → discuss with a peer → revote → instructor synthesis; use a question that requires reasoning.\
**Failure:** skipping individual commitment, using trivial recall, or treating majority opinion as proof.

## 9. Think–pair–share with artifact — B

**Use for:** widening participation and improving reasoning.\
**Pattern:** individual written thought → paired comparison → public sampling → synthesized artifact.\
**Failure:** immediate whole-class volunteering dominated by confident students.

## 10. Hinge question — B

**Use for:** deciding whether to advance, reteach, or differentiate.\
**Pattern:** one carefully designed question at a conceptual decision point; all students respond; each option maps to a misconception; use a preplanned response.\
**Failure:** asking a question whose result cannot change instruction.

## 11. Comparison of contrasting cases — B

**Use for:** concept formation, discrimination, and transfer.\
**Pattern:** juxtapose cases differing on the target feature; prompt noticing, explanation, rule formation, and a new case.\
**Failure:** comparing too many dimensions at once or revealing the rule before students inspect the cases.

## 12. Self-explanation — B

**Use for:** integrating steps, concepts, and prior knowledge.\
**Pattern:** prompt “why does this step follow?”, “what principle applies?”, or “how does this relate?”; sample and correct explanations.\
**Failure:** vague “explain your answer” prompts without criteria or feedback.

## 13. Elaborative interrogation — B

**Use for:** connecting factual claims to causal or conceptual knowledge.\
**Pattern:** ask why a fact is true or why it differs from a close alternative.\
**Failure:** using it when learners lack enough prior knowledge to generate a plausible explanation.

## 14. Deliberate practice loop — A/B

**Use for:** improving a specific component of complex performance.\
**Pattern:** precise goal → attempt → informative feedback → corrected attempt → varied use.\
**Failure:** undirected repetition or feedback without another attempt.

## 15. Exam wrapper/error analysis — B

**Use for:** metacognitive calibration and better preparation.\
**Pattern:** predict performance; classify errors; compare preparation to outcome; make a concrete next plan; revisit the plan.\
**Failure:** one-off reflection that has no connection to later action.

## 16. Two-stage exam — B

**Use for:** combining individual assurance with collaborative learning.\
**Pattern:** individual exam first, then selected questions in teams for a smaller proportion of points; debrief reasoning.\
**Failure:** allowing the group stage to replace independent evidence or using it for decisions requiring strictly individual certification.

## 17. Exemplars and comparative judgment — B

**Use for:** making quality visible and building evaluative judgment.\
**Pattern:** compare anonymized examples against criteria; identify features and tradeoffs; then plan or revise own work.\
**Failure:** showing only a perfect example that students imitate superficially.

## 18. Structured peer feedback — B

**Use for:** increasing feedback volume and developing judgment.\
**Pattern:** train on exemplars; limit criteria; require evidence and a next-step suggestion; include self-revision.\
**Failure:** untrained peers giving global praise or incorrect content guidance.

## 19. Utility-value connection — B

**Use for:** increasing perceived relevance, particularly for students with lower confidence.\
**Pattern:** students generate how a concept connects to their goals, communities, future decisions, or valued problems.\
**Failure:** instructor tells students that material is “important” without giving them cognitive ownership.

## 20. Choice within structure — B

**Use for:** autonomy without losing alignment.\
**Pattern:** offer bounded choices of topic, case, representation, or pathway while holding outcomes and criteria constant.\
**Failure:** unlimited choice that increases cognitive load, inequity, or marking unreliability.

## 21. Problem-based learning cycle — B/conditional

**Use for:** integration, inquiry, and professional reasoning after suitable foundations.\
**Pattern:** problem → facts → hypotheses → learning needs → self-directed study → application → reflection; facilitate with scaffolds.\
**Failure:** “figure it out” discovery for novices with no content support or assessment alignment.

## 22. Cognitive apprenticeship — B

**Use for:** disciplinary and professional practices that include tacit judgment.\
**Pattern:** model thinking aloud → coach → scaffold → articulate → reflect → explore independently.\
**Failure:** demonstrating only final performance while hiding expert decisions.

## 23. Segmenting and signaling — A/B

**Use for:** managing complex explanations and multimedia.\
**Pattern:** break into coherent learner-controlled units; signal structure and relevant relationships; coordinate text and visuals spatially/temporally.\
**Failure:** decorative cues, fragmented micro-content, or narration that duplicates on-screen text unnecessarily.

## 24. Process portfolio — B

**Use for:** longitudinal evidence, revision, and complex performance.\
**Pattern:** curated artifacts + rationale + feedback response + reflection + selected independent demonstration.\
**Failure:** a scrapbook of products with no construct model or scoring plan.

## 25. AI critique-and-revision — C/B emerging

**Use for:** learning evaluative judgment and responsible AI use.\
**Pattern:** student produces an initial analysis; AI generates an alternative; student verifies claims, locates errors/bias, revises, and documents decisions.\
**Failure:** students accept fluent output or the AI completes the entire target performance before the learner attempts it.

## 26. AI tutor with guardrails — C/B emerging

**Use for:** hints, explanation, and practice support outside staff hours.\
**Pattern:** Socratic hints, step limits, retrieval before answer, source verification, privacy controls, and escalation to a human.\
**Failure:** unrestricted answer generation that substitutes for practice and creates an illusion of competence.

## 27. Minute paper / exit ticket — B

**Use for:** rapid evidence of understanding and uncertainty.\
**Pattern:** one target-aligned prompt; sample before next session; respond visibly.\
**Failure:** repetitive “what was unclear?” prompts that generate no instructional action.

## 28. Specifications/checklist for routine criteria — B

**Use for:** transparent minimum standards and reduced grading noise.\
**Pattern:** observable criteria; exemplars; opportunities to revise; separate routine compliance from higher-order quality.\
**Failure:** binary grading of complex quality or excessive token systems that obscure learning.

## 29. Structured academic controversy — B

**Use for:** evidence evaluation, perspective taking, and argument.\
**Pattern:** prepare positions → advocate → reverse/represent opposing view → synthesize a reasoned judgment.\
**Failure:** unstructured debate rewarded for speed, confidence, or polarization.

## 30. Just-in-time teaching — B

**Use for:** adapting class to pre-class reasoning.\
**Pattern:** short pre-class task due early enough to review; instructor samples patterns; session begins with the most consequential needs.\
**Failure:** pre-class work used only for compliance points or too late to influence teaching.
