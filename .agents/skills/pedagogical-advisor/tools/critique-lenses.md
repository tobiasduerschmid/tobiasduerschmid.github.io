# Critique Lenses

Apply several lenses rather than giving a single global judgment. Report strengths first, then risks, then ranked revisions.

## Lens A — Purpose and alignment

- Are outcomes observable, consequential, and appropriately scoped?
- Do learning activities require the same type of thinking as the outcomes?
- Does assessment elicit the intended capability?
- Do criteria and grades reward the stated priorities?
- Is anything heavily taught but not assessed, or heavily assessed but not taught?

## Lens B — Learner and prerequisite fit

- What must learners already know or do?
- Are common misconceptions anticipated?
- Are novices being asked to discover too much without guidance?
- Are experts burdened by redundant support?
- Does the design account for variability in language, preparation, confidence, and access?

## Lens C — Cognitive architecture

- Is essential information selected and coherently organized?
- Is working memory burdened by split attention, redundancy, transience, or unnecessary complexity?
- Are component skills modeled before integration?
- Is guidance faded as competence grows?
- Does the design create desirable effort rather than arbitrary difficulty?

## Lens D — Student cognitive work

Classify the dominant activity with the ICAP heuristic:
- **Passive:** receive, listen, view;
- **Active:** manipulate or select without generating new meaning;
- **Constructive:** explain, predict, derive, map, create;
- **Interactive:** build meaning responsively with others.

Ask whether the overt activity reliably induces the intended mental work. Talking is not automatically interactive; clicking is not automatically active learning.

## Lens E — Practice, memory, and transfer

- Is important knowledge retrieved, not merely re-exposed?
- Is practice spaced and cumulative?
- Are problem types mixed when students must choose a strategy?
- Are contexts varied enough to support transfer?
- Are students prompted to compare cases and abstract principles?
- Is fluency mistaken for durable learning?

## Lens F — Assessment inference

- What claim will be made from the performance?
- What task features elicit evidence for that claim?
- How will evidence be interpreted?
- What alternative explanations threaten validity?
- Is sampling broad enough and scoring consistent enough for the stakes?
- Are consequential decisions supported by more than one weak measure?

## Lens G — Feedback and regulation

- Do learners know the goal, current state, and next action?
- Is there time and obligation to use feedback?
- Does the instructor receive feedback about learning and adapt?
- Are students developing self-assessment and evaluative judgment?
- Are errors analyzed rather than merely counted?

## Lens H — Motivation and climate

- Is the work valuable, relevant, and authentic without being gimmicky?
- Is challenge paired with a credible path to success?
- Is autonomy offered within clear structure?
- Are belonging, identity safety, respectful discourse, and help seeking supported?
- Do incentives accidentally reward shortcuts, avoidance, or performative compliance?

## Lens I — Inclusion, access, and fairness

- Which barriers are intrinsic to the capability and which are incidental?
- Are materials perceptible and navigable?
- Are instructions and expectations transparent?
- Are multiple paths offered where they preserve the construct?
- Are examples and participation structures inclusive?
- Could timing, language, technology, cost, or prior cultural familiarity distort the inference?

## Lens J — Feasibility and sustainability

- Is student workload credible?
- Is instructor/TA marking sustainable?
- Can the room, schedule, and technology support the plan?
- Are failure modes and contingencies specified?
- What will be removed to make room?
- Can the practice be maintained after the initial enthusiasm or grant ends?

## Lens K — GenAI coherence

- Does AI use preserve or bypass the intended cognitive work?
- Are permitted, required, and prohibited uses explicit by phase?
- Is verification, provenance, disclosure, privacy, and bias addressed?
- Is process evidence collected where products are easy to outsource?
- Is there proportionate independent evidence for high-stakes claims?
- Is the design robust to model improvement rather than dependent on current weaknesses?

## Lens L — Evidence and causal plausibility

- Is the recommendation supported by replicated evidence, conditional evidence, or analogy?
- Are moderators and implementation requirements stated?
- Is a correlational finding being used as causal proof?
- Is a broad label hiding many different implementations?
- Is the proposal testable through local evidence?

## Critique output format

1. **What is already strong**
2. **Central design diagnosis**
3. **Highest-priority revisions** — no more than five, ranked
4. **Secondary refinements**
5. **Evidence strength and caveats**
6. **A revised excerpt or model**
7. **Questions that materially change the design**
