# Myths, Controversies, and Boundaries

## Learning styles matching — avoid

People have preferences and strengths, but evidence does not support matching instruction to a visual/auditory/kinesthetic style to improve learning. Choose representations based on content and provide accessible multimodal support.

## Fixed attention-span rule — avoid

There is no universal rule that attention expires after 8, 10, or 15 minutes. Segment by cognitive function and use evidence of processing.

## Direct instruction versus constructivism — false dichotomy

Learners construct understanding, and novices often need explicit explanation, modeling, and guidance. Effective design combines instruction with guided active sense-making and fades support.

## Discovery learning — qualify

Unassisted discovery generally underperforms explicit or guided approaches for novices. Guided inquiry, generation, and productive failure can help under suitable conditions.

## Active learning means group work — false

Active learning is target-relevant cognitive activity. Group work can be passive or inequitable; individual retrieval can be highly active.

## More feedback is better — false

Feedback effects are heterogeneous. Feedback must be interpretable, actionable, timely, and used. Excess comments can overwhelm.

## Rereading and highlighting — limited

They can orient attention or support later work, but are weak as primary learning strategies. Replace much of their use with retrieval, explanation, and spaced practice.

## Desirable difficulty means make it hard — false

Only difficulties that improve later learning are desirable. Confusion, inaccessibility, overload, and excessive workload are not.

## Critical thinking is generic — qualify

General dispositions and strategies matter, but effective critical thinking relies heavily on domain knowledge and disciplinary standards.

## Authentic assessment is always superior — false

Authentic tasks may improve construct representation but can introduce uncontrolled assistance, scoring inconsistency, and inequitable resources. Use complementary evidence.

## Effect-size rankings determine practice — false

Average effects from different constructs, outcomes, and comparisons are not directly actionable. Examine direct evidence and context.

## Growth mindset is a high-impact intervention for all — contested

Reviews find small, heterogeneous, or bias-sensitive effects. Build a genuine improvement system rather than relying on slogans.

## UDL means every student chooses everything — false

UDL anticipates barriers and supports agency. Choices must remain coherent with the learning construct and manageable.

## Lecture is always bad — false

Clear, well-structured explanation is valuable. Long passive exposure without processing and feedback is the problem.

## Tests only measure learning — false

Retrieval tests can also create learning. But testing can narrow curriculum and create threat when poorly designed.

## Homework builds character — unsupported as a primary rationale

Assign work for a defined learning function. Claims about responsibility or discipline require evidence and should not justify excessive burden.

## AI-proof assignments — unsustainable

Model capabilities change. Redesign evidence of learning and include process, verification, oral, and controlled components.

## AI detectors prove misconduct — false

Detection tools have error and bias risks. Use multiple evidence sources and procedural fairness.

## Neuroscience guarantees classroom methods — avoid overreach

Neuroscience can constrain and enrich learning theory, but many classroom recommendations are supported more directly by cognitive and educational research. Reject “brain-based” marketing without educational evidence.

## Boundaries

This skill supports pedagogical design, not:
- legal determinations about accommodations or misconduct;
- clinical diagnosis;
- formal psychometric certification;
- accreditation decisions without current standards;
- institutional policy approval;
- guaranteed causal claims from local course data.

Use relevant experts and current policy when stakes require.
