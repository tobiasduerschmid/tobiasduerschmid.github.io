# Homework and Assignment Design

Homework should create learning that cannot be achieved as efficiently during contact time. It should not be unbounded content overflow, punishment, or evidence of compliance.

## Define the function

Possible functions:
- prepare prior knowledge;
- retrieve and consolidate;
- practice a component;
- discriminate among problem types;
- extend to a new context;
- gather data or observations;
- create a draft or prototype;
- integrate across modules;
- support reflection and planning;
- collaborate;
- produce summative evidence.

State the function in student-facing language.

## Design for learning, not volume

A strong homework set:
- aligns with an outcome;
- has a manageable time budget;
- includes clear instructions;
- provides necessary models or resources;
- begins at an achievable level;
- includes challenge and variation;
- provides feedback or self-check;
- returns in later learning;
- avoids unnecessary production work;
- makes allowed collaboration and AI use explicit.

## Problem sets

A learning-oriented sequence:
1. retrieval of prerequisites;
2. worked example or model;
3. near problem;
4. completion problem;
5. mixed strategy-selection problems;
6. error diagnosis;
7. explanation item;
8. transfer problem;
9. self-check and reflection.

Avoid 20 nearly identical problems when a smaller varied set plus feedback would be more useful.

## Reading assignments

Do not assign “read chapter 6” without a learning task. Use:
- guiding questions;
- prediction;
- retrieval from prior reading;
- concept map;
- claim/evidence table;
- annotation of a specific feature;
- preclass quiz;
- confusion/question submission;
- comparison with a case.

Keep accountability low stakes and focused on preparation.

## Writing assignments

Specify:
- purpose and audience;
- genre and disciplinary function;
- stages;
- source expectations;
- criteria;
- models;
- feedback and revision;
- AI policy;
- process evidence.

Teach component skills: question formation, evidence selection, organization, synthesis, citation, revision. Do not assume students can infer disciplinary writing conventions.

## Projects

Break into meaningful milestones:
- proposal;
- plan;
- source/data audit;
- prototype;
- peer critique;
- revision;
- final product;
- defense or presentation;
- reflection.

Each milestone should produce evidence and reduce late failure. Avoid micro-deadlines that become compliance bureaucracy.

## Homework feedback

Options:
- automated correctness with explanation;
- model solutions after submission;
- peer comparison;
- selected instructor feedback;
- in-class error analysis;
- self-correction;
- resubmission;
- oral check;
- cumulative quiz.

Do not collect work that no one uses.

## Grading homework

Homework often serves practice, so:
- use low stakes;
- allow drops or recovery;
- grade completion only when completion is the intended behavior, not as a proxy for mastery;
- sample items for quality;
- use mastery/retry for foundational skills;
- distinguish collaboration from copying;
- avoid making access to outside help determine grades.

High homework weight can measure resources and compliance more than learning.

## Workload estimation

Estimate:
- reading speed and density;
- problem-solving time;
- tool setup;
- research;
- writing and revision;
- coordination;
- accessibility needs.

Pilot with a TA or student and collect actual time reports. If students routinely exceed the budget, revise the design rather than attributing the problem to poor time management.

## Equity

Consider:
- quiet space;
- device and internet;
- paid work and care;
- time zones;
- disability;
- language;
- software access;
- group scheduling;
- prior preparation;
- expensive materials.

Provide alternatives and on-campus resources. Design tasks that can be completed without privileged outside support.

## GenAI

For each assignment specify:
- prohibited, permitted, or required uses;
- purpose;
- disclosure;
- verification;
- source handling;
- privacy;
- what the student must do independently;
- process evidence;
- how competence will be assured.

Prefer assignments where AI use can be analyzed, critiqued, or incorporated transparently. Do not assume customization alone makes a task AI-resistant.

## Academic integrity

Integrity grows from:
- meaningful work;
- manageable workload;
- clear rules;
- adequate preparation;
- staged evidence;
- opportunity for help;
- assessment aligned with learning;
- consistent enforcement.

Detection is a weak substitute for design.

## Homework quality questions

- Why is this outside class?
- What learning process will occur?
- Is the amount justified?
- What feedback follows?
- Will it recur?
- Are prerequisites and models sufficient?
- Does grading fit practice purpose?
- Are access barriers minimized?
- Is collaboration/AI policy clear?
- How will we know it worked?
