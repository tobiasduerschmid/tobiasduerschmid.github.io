# Discipline and Learner-Level Adaptation

Evidence-based principles must be translated into the epistemology and practices of a discipline.

## Expertise continuum

### Novice
Needs:
- explicit structure;
- prerequisite activation;
- worked examples;
- limited choices;
- guided practice;
- frequent feedback;
- clear criteria;
- concrete representations.

### Intermediate
Needs:
- fading;
- mixed practice;
- comparison;
- increased independence;
- integration;
- error diagnosis;
- authentic cases.

### Advanced
Needs:
- complex ambiguity;
- independent inquiry;
- critique of methods;
- adaptive performance;
- professional standards;
- mentoring and peer review.

Do not treat age or course number as a perfect proxy; use performance evidence.

## STEM

Emphasize:
- conceptual models plus procedures;
- multiple representations;
- worked examples and fading;
- prediction and demonstration;
- mixed problem selection;
- explanation of assumptions;
- labs as inquiry with guidance;
- cumulative retrieval;
- authentic data and uncertainty.

Avoid problem sets consisting only of repeated templates.

## Humanities

Emphasize:
- close reading;
- interpretation;
- contextual knowledge;
- argument;
- comparison of perspectives;
- source criticism;
- disciplinary evidence;
- writing as thinking;
- seminar protocols;
- iterative drafts.

Retrieval remains important for factual and conceptual resources that enable analysis.

## Social sciences

Emphasize:
- theory–evidence mapping;
- causal reasoning;
- methods;
- competing explanations;
- data interpretation;
- application to cases;
- ethical and policy judgment;
- uncertainty.

## Professional education

Emphasize:
- cases;
- simulations;
- decision thresholds;
- procedures;
- communication;
- ethics;
- feedback;
- reflection;
- transfer to practice;
- supervised authentic performance.

Assess reasoning and action, not only written products.

## Laboratory and studio

Use:
- demonstrations and safety modeling;
- deliberate practice;
- observation criteria;
- critique;
- iterative production;
- documentation;
- oral explanation;
- increasingly independent design.

## Computing

Distinguish:
- code writing;
- code reading;
- debugging;
- decomposition;
- design;
- testing;
- algorithmic reasoning;
- tool use;
- AI-assisted development;
- ethical judgment.

In a GenAI context, assess process, explanation, testing, adaptation, and independent foundations.

## Writing-intensive courses

Sequence:
- analyze models;
- practice components;
- plan;
- draft;
- receive feedback;
- revise;
- reflect;
- transfer to a new genre/audience.

Do not use rubrics as a substitute for teaching writing.

## Quantitative courses

Use:
- conceptual questions;
- estimation;
- interpretation;
- representations;
- worked examples;
- mixed problem sets;
- error analysis;
- explanation;
- authentic data.

## Large introductory courses

Prioritize:
- essential outcomes;
- common routines;
- active response systems;
- high-structure practice;
- cumulative low-stakes assessment;
- TA calibration;
- transparent communication;
- early support.

## Seminars

Prioritize:
- prepared evidence;
- student questions;
- structured discussion;
- rotating facilitation;
- synthesis;
- research or writing;
- intellectual community.

## Online asynchronous

Prioritize:
- explicit navigation;
- short learning cycles;
- interaction with purpose;
- timely feedback;
- accessible media;
- workload clarity;
- presence;
- cumulative practice.

## Adaptation rule

Do not ask “Which method works in this discipline?” Ask:
- What does competent performance look like?
- What knowledge structures support it?
- What novices get wrong?
- Which practice exposes the relevant thinking?
- What evidence can be observed?
- What tools and norms shape the practice?
