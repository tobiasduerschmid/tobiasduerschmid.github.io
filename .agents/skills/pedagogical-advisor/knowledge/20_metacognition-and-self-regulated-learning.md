# Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning

## Cycle

Self-regulated learning includes:
1. analyze task;
2. set goals;
3. assess prior knowledge;
4. choose strategy;
5. allocate time and resources;
6. act;
7. monitor;
8. seek feedback/help;
9. adjust;
10. evaluate and transfer.

Experts often perform this implicitly; students need explicit models and repeated prompts.

## Calibration

Students are poor judges when they rely on familiarity and fluency. Improve calibration through:
- prediction before assessment;
- confidence ratings;
- closed-book retrieval;
- comparison of expected and actual performance;
- error classification;
- delayed checks;
- explanation;
- strategy logs linked to evidence.

## Strategy instruction

Teach when and why to use:
- retrieval;
- spacing;
- interleaving;
- elaboration;
- self-explanation;
- planning;
- monitoring;
- help seeking;
- note organization;
- source evaluation.

A list of study tips is insufficient. Model, practice, feedback, and fade prompts.

## Reflection

Good reflection cites evidence and changes future action.

Weak: “I need to study harder.”

Stronger: “I overestimated mastery because I reread notes. On transfer items I failed to choose the relevant model. Before the next assessment I will complete two mixed closed-book sets on separate days, classify each problem before solving, and check errors against the decision rules.”

## Exam wrappers and learning journals

Use short, repeated instruments:
- preparation method and time;
- predicted result;
- error categories;
- feedback interpretation;
- next strategy;
- follow-up evidence.

Do not create lengthy reflective paperwork that competes with practice.

## Help seeking

Teach:
- how to recognize being stuck;
- what attempt to make first;
- how to ask a specific question;
- when to use peers, instructor, resources, or AI;
- how to verify help;
- how to return to independent performance.

## Planning supports

Use:
- milestone calendars;
- task decomposition;
- time estimates;
- checklists;
- exemplars;
- progress dashboards;
- implementation intentions;
- reminders that fade.

## AI and metacognition

AI can mask gaps by producing fluent output. Require:
- learner attempt before AI;
- prediction;
- explanation of accepted/rejected suggestions;
- error checking;
- unaided retrieval;
- evidence of revision decisions;
- reflection on reliance.

## Evidence stance

Metacognitive prompts and strategy instruction are **B**, strongest when embedded in domain tasks and linked to feedback. Generic reflection detached from performance is **D**.
