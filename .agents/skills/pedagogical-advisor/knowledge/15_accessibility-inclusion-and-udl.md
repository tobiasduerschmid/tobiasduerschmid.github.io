# Accessibility, Inclusion, and Universal Design for Learning

## Design principle

Reduce barriers that are irrelevant to the learning goal while preserving the essential construct and level of challenge. Accessibility is not an afterthought or a reduction in rigor.

## Learner variability

Anticipate differences in:
- sensory access;
- mobility;
- language;
- background knowledge;
- attention and executive function;
- processing speed;
- cultural experience;
- technology;
- time;
- confidence;
- communication;
- prior educational opportunity.

Do not convert variability into fixed learner types.

## UDL 3.0 as a design heuristic

CAST’s UDL Guidelines 3.0 organize options across:
- engagement;
- representation;
- action and expression.

The 2024 update emphasizes learner agency, identity, bias, belonging, and systemic exclusion. Use UDL to ask where unnecessary barriers exist, not as a checklist that requires every option in every lesson.

## Multiple means—when appropriate

### Engagement
- clarify purpose;
- optimize relevant choice;
- calibrate challenge and support;
- foster collaboration and belonging;
- reduce threats and distractions;
- support persistence and reflection.

### Representation
- accessible text, audio, captions, transcripts, alt text;
- clear language and notation;
- multiple examples and representations;
- highlighted structure;
- background knowledge support;
- stable reference materials.

### Action and expression
- accessible tools;
- planning supports;
- options for response when modality is not the target;
- assistive technology compatibility;
- multiple stages and feedback;
- alternative navigation and input.

## Construct relevance

Ask what is essential:
- If oral presentation is the outcome, an essay is not equivalent.
- If evidence-based reasoning is the outcome, oral, written, or visual forms may be comparable with common criteria.
- If rapid emergency response is essential, time may be part of the construct.
- If speed is not essential, strict time can be an irrelevant barrier.

Document these decisions.

## Accommodations

Accommodations provide access for individual needs and legal obligations. They may include:
- extended time;
- reduced-distraction setting;
- alternate format;
- assistive technology;
- captioning/interpreting;
- flexible attendance;
- breaks;
- note-taking support.

Coordinate with institutional services. Do not require students to disclose private details beyond established processes.

## Accessible materials

Check:
- headings and reading order;
- contrast;
- font size;
- meaningful link text;
- captions and transcripts;
- alt text;
- table headers;
- keyboard navigation;
- accessible equations;
- color not used alone;
- document language;
- downloadable formats;
- mobile compatibility.

## Language and multilingual learners

Support:
- key vocabulary;
- explicit discourse structures;
- models and sentence frames where useful;
- additional processing time;
- opportunities to rehearse;
- visual and conceptual supports;
- assessment that separates language proficiency from the target when appropriate.

Do not simplify disciplinary thinking. Make the language of that thinking teachable.

## Inclusive content and participation

Use:
- varied names, contexts, histories, and contributions;
- critical examination of whose knowledge is represented;
- community knowledge and examples;
- protocols that distribute voice;
- options for private preparation before public response;
- explicit norms for disagreement.

Avoid token representation and assumptions that a student speaks for a group.

## Flexibility versus overload

Too many options can burden learners and instructors. Offer a small set of high-value pathways with common goals and criteria.

## Equity audit

- Who has easy access to this task?
- Who carries extra hidden work?
- Does the task assume money, time, travel, software, or cultural familiarity?
- Are group schedules feasible?
- Are examples and criteria interpretable?
- Are participation and feedback distributed?
- Do policies have unequal consequences?
- Is data disaggregated to reveal patterns?

## Evidence stance

Accessibility standards and proactive barrier reduction are non-negotiable professional practice. UDL is a well-developed framework, but claims that implementing a generic UDL package automatically raises achievement should be treated as **B/C** and evaluated locally. Use specific, mechanism-linked design choices rather than branding alone.
