# Course Design

Course design is the construction of a coherent learning system across time. A syllabus is one representation of that system, not the system itself.

## Start with consequential capabilities

Identify a small set of course-level outcomes that matter beyond the final exam. They may include:
- foundational knowledge available from memory;
- conceptual models;
- disciplinary methods and standards of evidence;
- problem solving and decision making;
- interpretation and critique;
- production or performance;
- communication;
- ethical and professional judgment;
- metacognitive and collaborative capabilities;
- transfer to later courses, practice, or public life.

Separate “important to encounter” from “must be able to use.” Content coverage expands easily; learning time does not.

## Learner analysis

Estimate:
- prerequisite knowledge and variability;
- common misconceptions;
- experience with disciplinary practices;
- language and academic-literacy demands;
- confidence, value, and likely threat;
- technology and time access;
- prior exposure to assessment formats;
- learner goals and future uses.

Use diagnostic evidence rather than faculty intuition alone. Expert blind spots make familiar component skills invisible.

## Build a learning progression

A progression should specify:
- target performance;
- dimensions of development;
- intermediate levels;
- learning performances at each level;
- evidence that indicates movement.

Sequence can move:
- concrete to abstract;
- simple interactions to complex systems;
- worked examples to completion tasks to independent problems;
- isolated components to integration;
- familiar contexts to novel transfer;
- constrained judgment to open-ended judgment;
- instructor modeling to student self-regulation.

Do not sequence solely by textbook chapter or disciplinary history unless that order supports learning.

## Module coherence

Each module should answer:
- What capability advances?
- What prior learning is retrieved?
- What new model, method, or distinction is introduced?
- What guided practice occurs?
- What feedback is generated?
- How is learning integrated with earlier modules?
- What evidence informs the next step?
- What remains for later spacing and transfer?

Modules should be connected by recurring questions, models, cases, or practices. Students need help seeing connections that experts perceive automatically.

## Assessment architecture

Use a balanced system:
- **diagnostic:** prerequisites and misconceptions;
- **formative:** frequent, low-stakes evidence that changes action;
- **practice:** effortful attempts with feedback;
- **summative:** defensible sampling of course outcomes;
- **integrative:** cumulative tasks across modules;
- **transfer:** novel or authentic applications;
- **metacognitive:** prediction, monitoring, error analysis, and planning.

High-stakes assessments should not be the first time students encounter the required cognitive demand.

## Practice architecture across the term

Map where each major capability is:
- introduced;
- modeled;
- practiced with support;
- practiced independently;
- retrieved after delay;
- varied or interleaved;
- integrated;
- assessed;
- revisited after feedback.

Important capabilities need multiple opportunities. One assignment cannot simultaneously introduce, practice, and certify a complex skill without substantial scaffolding.

## Course climate and motivation

Design:
- a credible invitation into the discipline;
- transparent purposes and expectations;
- early achievable challenge;
- normalizing of help seeking and revision;
- respectful participation norms;
- evidence that improvement is possible through strategies;
- examples and applications that widen, not narrow, who can see relevance;
- consistent policies and predictable routines.

Avoid empty motivational messaging. Structure and successful practice create expectancy.

## Workload

Estimate student time for:
- reading or viewing;
- retrieval/preparation;
- problem solving;
- writing;
- research;
- group coordination;
- revision;
- exam preparation.

Estimate instructor/TA time for:
- preparation;
- facilitation;
- feedback;
- grading;
- accommodation;
- moderation;
- data review.

Use a workload budget. If a new activity is added, identify what it replaces.

## Large-course design

Large classes can use evidence-based practices when they include:
- response systems or structured commitment;
- peer instruction;
- short worksheets or tutorials;
- team-based routines;
- automated or sampled feedback;
- exam blueprints and item banks;
- clear communication and predictable cycles;
- TA calibration;
- rapid classroom assessment.

The challenge is not class size alone; it is orchestration, accountability, feedback, and logistics.

## Online and hybrid design

Avoid converting contact hours into videos. Organize around learning cycles:
- orient;
- activate;
- explain/model;
- practice;
- interact;
- receive feedback;
- revise;
- synthesize.

Presence is designed through timely communication, visible facilitation, peer interaction, and feedback—not video quantity.

## GenAI-aware course architecture

At the course level:
- identify capabilities students must retain unaided;
- identify capabilities that include competent AI use;
- decide where AI is prohibited, permitted, or required;
- teach verification, attribution, privacy, bias, and judgment;
- create repeated process evidence;
- include observable or controlled demonstrations where assurance is necessary;
- align policies across assignments;
- avoid placing all assurance in a single final exam.

## Course review questions

- Are outcomes few enough to drive decisions?
- Is the course organized around disciplinary structure rather than coverage?
- Are prerequisites diagnosed?
- Does each major outcome receive sufficient practice and feedback?
- Does assessment sample the stated outcomes?
- Are retrieval and spacing visible across the calendar?
- Are students prepared for high-demand tasks?
- Can the marking system sustain the design?
- Are policies and criteria coherent?
- Are access barriers anticipated?
- Is there a plan to learn from the course?
