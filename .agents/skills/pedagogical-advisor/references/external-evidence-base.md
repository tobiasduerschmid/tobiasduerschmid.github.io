# External Evidence Base

**Currency date:** 2026-07-18. This layer supplements the project corpus with later syntheses, primary studies, standards, and current guidance. For rapidly changing topics, rerun `procedures/14_evidence-update-workflow.md`.

## Selection policy

Prefer:
1. consensus reports and official standards;
2. systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
3. strong multi-site or field experiments;
4. primary studies that clarify a mechanism or new technology;
5. official current guidance for policy-sensitive topics.

Do not let a recent, newsworthy study automatically outweigh a larger and more mature evidence base.

## Learning across learners, contexts, and cultures

### National Academies, *How People Learn II* (2018)

Extends the earlier framework by emphasizing culture, context, development across the lifespan, motivation, identity, and technology. Use it to prevent an overly individual cognitive account of learning. DOI: `10.17226/24783`.

## Retrieval, spacing, and interleaving

### IES practice guide, *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning* (2007)

Translates research into recommendations including spacing, interleaving worked examples and problems, combining graphics with verbal descriptions, connecting abstract and concrete representations, quizzing, and deep explanatory questions. Evidence ratings in the guide reflect the literature available at publication.

### Cepeda et al. (2006), distributed practice

Large review supporting spacing across verbal learning tasks and identifying retention interval as a central moderator. DOI: `10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354`.

### Adesope et al. (2017), practice testing

Meta-analysis supports practice testing across learning conditions and highlights design moderators. DOI: `10.3102/0034654316689306`.

### Brunmair and Richter (2019), interleaving

Meta-analysis finds that similarity and task characteristics matter; interleaving is most useful where learners must discriminate categories or methods, not as indiscriminate mixing. DOI: `10.1037/bul0000209`.

## Active and collaborative learning

### Freeman et al. (2014)

Project source; still foundational for undergraduate STEM active learning. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1319030111`.

### Theobald et al. (2020)

Meta-analysis reports that active learning can narrow achievement gaps for underrepresented students in undergraduate STEM, particularly with high-intensity, inclusive implementation. Do not infer that any activity is equitable by label alone. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1916903117`.

### Deslauriers et al. (2019)

Randomized classroom study distinguishes actual learning from the feeling of learning: students in active conditions learned more while sometimes feeling they learned less. Explain desirable effort and use objective evidence. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1821936116`.

### Chi and Wylie (2014), ICAP

Framework differentiates passive, active, constructive, and interactive engagement by the cognitive products and interaction patterns they induce. Use as a design heuristic, not a guarantee based on overt behavior. DOI: `10.1080/00461520.2014.965823`.

### Springer, Stanne, and Donovan (1999)

Meta-analysis supports small-group learning in undergraduate science, mathematics, engineering, and technology for achievement, attitudes, and persistence. Effective collaboration still depends on task design, accountability, and facilitation. DOI: `10.3102/00346543069001021`.

## Guidance and cognitive load

### Atkinson et al. (2000), worked examples

Review derives design principles for worked examples, including integration of representations and transition toward problem solving. DOI: `10.3102/00346543070002181`.

### Alfieri et al. (2011), discovery learning

Meta-analysis distinguishes unassisted discovery from enhanced/guided discovery. Unassisted discovery generally underperforms explicit instruction; feedback, worked examples, scaffolding, and elicited explanations improve inquiry. DOI: `10.1037/a0021017`.

### Sweller, van Merriënboer, and Paas (2019)

Twenty-year update on cognitive architecture and instructional design. Reinforces limited working-memory constraints, schema acquisition, and expertise-dependent design while documenting theoretical development. DOI: `10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5`.

## Feedback, rubrics, and evaluative judgment

### Wisniewski, Zierer, and Hattie (2020)

Meta-analysis finds feedback effects vary substantially by information content and context. Feedback is not a single treatment; design around task/process/self-regulation information and a subsequent opportunity to act. DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087`.

### Panadero and Jönsson (2013)

Review suggests formative rubric use may support performance and self-regulation, with moderators such as student involvement, exemplars, self/peer assessment, and how criteria are used. DOI: `10.1016/j.edurev.2013.01.002`.

### AERA, APA, and NCME, *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014)

Primary standards source for validity, reliability/precision, fairness, testing operations, scoring, and appropriate uses. Validity concerns interpretations and uses of scores, not a test as a permanently valid object.

## Motivation, mindset, and belonging

### Lazowski and Hulleman (2016)

Meta-analysis of motivation interventions shows positive average effects but emphasizes theoretically targeted design, implementation fidelity, and context. DOI: `10.3102/0034654315617832`.

### Sisk et al. (2018)

Two meta-analyses found weak associations between growth mindset and achievement and small average intervention effects, with possible benefits for some high-risk groups but limited evidence. Do not sell mindset messaging as a universal achievement intervention. DOI: `10.1177/0956797617739704`.

### Walton et al. (2023)

Large multi-institution randomized study of a brief social-belonging intervention found context- and group-dependent effects on first-year progress. Institutional affordances and local belonging barriers moderate whether a targeted intervention works. DOI: `10.1126/science.ade4420` (with a later erratum).

## Accessibility and Universal Design for Learning

### CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 (2024)

Current framework emphasizes learner agency and multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression, with greater attention to bias and exclusion. Treat UDL as a design framework and barrier-analysis tool, not a single intervention with one effect size. Preserve the construct being learned or assessed.

## Generative AI and assessment reform

### Franklin et al. (2025)

Project source synthesizing computing-education challenges, uses, and emerging course designs. DOI: `10.1017/9781009581738`.

### Bastani et al. (2025), *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning*

Field evidence in high-school mathematics found unrestricted GPT access could improve assisted practice performance while impairing later unaided performance; tutor-like guardrails mitigated negative learning effects. The key design question is whether AI supports or substitutes for productive effort. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.2422633122`.

### UNESCO, *Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research* (2023; updated 2026)

Human-centered policy guidance covering capacity, inclusion, privacy, age-appropriate use, validation, and governance. Use for policy and ethical considerations rather than effect estimates.

### TEQSA assessment-reform series (2023–2026)

- *Assessment Reform for the Age of Artificial Intelligence* — principles and propositions.
- *Enacting Assessment Reform in a Time of Artificial Intelligence* (2025) — implementation examples.
- *Assuring Quality Learning in a Gen AI-Integrated Future: The Role of Adaptive Capabilities* (2026) — evaluative judgment, critical thinking, ethical reasoning, and learning assurance.

The series favors programmatic assessment reform over endless “AI-proofing,” and distinguishes development of AI-era capabilities from assurance that learning occurred.

## Update triggers

Run a new search when:
- a recommendation depends on current model capability;
- an institution, regulator, or accreditor has changed policy;
- a new accessibility standard or UDL revision is released;
- a major meta-analysis challenges an existing evidence label;
- an assessment technology changes what can be independently observed;
- a claim uses “current,” “latest,” “now,” or “as of” language.
