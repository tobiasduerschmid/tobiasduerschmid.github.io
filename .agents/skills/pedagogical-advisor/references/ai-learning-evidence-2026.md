This is a targeted primary-source evidence update, not an exhaustive systematic review. Use with `knowledge/25_ai-literacy-and-learning-safeguards.md`. Evidence grades apply to the stated inference, not to a technology or author generally. The instructional recipes in that chapter are design syntheses that require local evaluation; none should be advertised as an experimentally validated package.

## Outcome discipline

Record separately: assisted task performance; immediate independent performance; delayed independent retention; transfer to a new task; self-report; engagement/usage; teacher workload; and distributional effects. A positive result in one column does not fill the others. Confidence, enjoyment, source accuracy, and prompt compliance are useful process measures, not substitutes for learning. A nonsignificant contrast is not an equivalence or noninferiority result. Avoid averaging incompatible interventions and endpoints into an “AI effectiveness” number.

## AI26-BAS — Safeguards can prevent harm without demonstrating learning gains

**Source:** Hamsa Bastani, Osbert Bastani, Alp Sungu, Haosen Ge, Özge Kabakcı, and Rei Mariman (2025), *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics*, PNAS 122(26), e2422633122. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/).

**Design/access:** Full main text and correction checked. Preregistered classroom-randomized trial in one Turkish high school, grades 9–11, nearly 1,000 students, four 90-minute sessions. Main analysis excludes honors classrooms; 2,848 student-session observations are not 2,848 unique students. Comparator: books/notes; interventions: GPT Base or teacher-informed GPT Tutor.

**Measured:** Assisted practice improved 48%/127% relative to control. Subsequent closed-resource exam scores fell 5.4 percentage points with Base, about 17% relative to the control mean. Tutor's −0.4-point estimate was nonsignificant.

**Inference/limits — B:** Tool design matters; do not describe Tutor as raising independent achievement. Hints and teacher-provided solutions/error guidance were bundled, so their separate causal contributions are unidentified. These were immediate, similar-problem exams, not long-term retention. [Correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403119/) concerns an author affiliation, not results.

## AI26-KES — Strong short-lesson result for a carefully constructed tutor

**Source:** Greg Kestin, Kelly Miller, Anna Klales, Timothy Milbourne, and Gregorio Ponti (2025), *AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting*, Scientific Reports 15, 17458. [DOI/full text](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6).

**Design/access:** Full main text inspected. Harvard introductory physics, 233 enrolled/194 study-eligible students; two-lesson randomized crossover preserving existing peer groups. At-home structured tutor with prepared solutions, videos, and pacing versus established in-class active learning on matched material.

**Measured:** Immediate separate posttests favored AI; reported adjusted linear-regression effect 0.63 SD. Median AI study time was 49 minutes versus an assumed 60 instructional minutes in class. Aggregate condition observations are not unique participant counts.

**Inference/limits — B for this setting, C for transfer:** This tests an instructional bundle, not a bare chatbot or an isolated prompt. One institution, two physics topics, immediate outcomes, different learning settings, and no delayed-retention demonstration. Do not claim universal doubling of learning, teacher replacement, or equal examination supervision without checking supplements. Preserve the authors' endpoint and model-specific effect-size definitions.

## AI26-COP — Assist the tutor and measure student outcomes

**Source:** Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, and Dorottya Demszky, *Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise*, EdWorkingPaper 24-1054, November 2025 revision. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26300/81nh-8262); [revised full text](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai24_1054_v2.pdf).

**Design/access:** Revised main methods/results and selected appendices inspected. Preregistered live mathematics tutoring RCT; 783 active tutors, approximately 1,000 K–12 students from underserved communities in one US district, March–May 2024. Randomized tutor access to suggestions versus normal human tutoring.

**Measured:** Unconditional exit-ticket passing rose approximately 4 percentage points; lower-rated tutors' students gained about 9 points. Student perception and participation outcomes were nonsignificant.

**Inference/limits — B/C:** Supports teacher-facing coaching, not autonomous replacement. Session mastery is not demonstrated year-long retention; projected annual test effects are extrapolations. The initial [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017) reports 900 tutors/1,800 students; do not mix that sample with revised analyses. The 783 count concerns active tutors, not the initial randomization pool. Model-token costs omit full institutional implementation costs.

## AI26-THO — A semester-long null result survives publication

**Source:** Andrew Thoeni and Luke K. Fryer (2026), *AI chatbots in higher education: Comparing expectations to evidence*, Computers in Human Behavior Reports 22, 101061. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101061); [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958826001351); [author-institution metadata](https://scholars.unf.edu/en/publications/ai-chatbots-in-higher-education-comparing-expectations-to-evidenc/).

**Design/access:** Publisher abstract and institutional publication metadata verified; full publisher methods inaccessible in this retrieval. Semester-long randomized field experiment, approximately 500 undergraduates in in-person/asynchronous modalities; course-grounded RAG chatbot access versus control.

**Measured:** No statistically significant effect on measured interest, self-efficacy, engagement, or academic achievement.

**Inference/limits — B/C:** Accurate course retrieval and availability are insufficient guarantees. Do not infer zero effect, equivalence, precise power, or an exact analytic N from the abstract. Check uptake, assignment details, attrition, test conditions, and confidence intervals before making a procurement decision. The [2025 preprint](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/24tg7_v1) uses a different title and approximate sample description; treat it as the same research lineage, not independent replication. Published online April 17, 2026; issue May 2026.

## AI26-CON — Contrary evidence: general-purpose access can support unaided learning

**Source:** Zara Contractor and Germán Reyes (2026), *Experimental Evidence on the Learning Impact of Generative AI*. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08849v1); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.08849).

**Design/access:** Preprint abstract and main methods/results inspected. Middlebury undergraduates, spring 2025: 256 signed up, 211 attended the first session, 204 both. Randomized permission/access to general-purpose AI during topic learning and analytical writing versus no AI; proctored independent tests immediately and approximately one week later.

**Measured:** Abstract reports a 0.27-SD immediate test gain persisting at follow-up. Delayed essay improvements varied by quality dimension. Augmentation/automation usage subgroups were observational, not independently randomized.

**Inference/limits — C:** Counters a universal claim that open AI access necessarily harms learning. Short duration, one selective institution, brief tests, and some protocol violations constrain inference. HTML numerical substitutions are visibly corrupted in places; retain verified abstract estimates and verify PDF tables before quoting further numbers. Submission record is July 9; retrieved manuscript displays August 24, 2026. No peer-reviewed status established.

## AI26-FUT — Theory-informed prompts alone need not improve learning

**Source:** Tim Fütterer, Lisa Bardach, Jochen Kuhn, Stefan Daniel Keller, and Peter Gerjets (2026), *Enhancing School Students' Self-Regulated Learning through Generative AI Support: A Randomized Controlled Trial*, Educational Psychology Review 38, 42. [DOI/full text](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-026-10133-8).

**Design/access:** Publisher abstract, intervention description, and conclusion inspected. Preregistered three-arm RCT, 371 students in grades 7–9, six 45-minute physics/English sessions. Utility-value reflection or cognitive-strategy prompting compared with standard ChatGPT.

**Measured:** Utility-value development favored that intervention over cognitive-strategy prompting. Neither intervention showed significant advantages over control for domain knowledge, effort, or elaboration-based strategy use. Meaningful interaction findings were exploratory.

**Inference/limits — B for tested contrasts:** Do not advertise generic “metacognitive prompts” as reliably effective. No AI-free arm means this does not estimate AI versus no AI. Favorable within-group change does not establish superiority over another condition. Limited duration and implementation fidelity matter; motivation and knowledge outcomes must remain separate. Published April 14, 2026.

## AI26-DIO — Promising SRL scaffolding with careful statistical interpretation

**Source:** Elena-Alexandra Diordiev, Valdemar Aksel Stenberdt, and Guido Makransky (2025), *Designing Emotionally Intelligent Generative AI: Enhancing Self-Regulated Learning and Socio-Emotional Outcomes in Education*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.23668/psycharchives.16423); [primary preprint record](https://www.psycharchives.org/en/item/4fad674e-fb9c-40f8-8bb0-050ec592671b).

**Design/access:** Author-deposited abstract/metadata inspected; linked manuscript download failed. Preregistered randomized study, 144 high-school students: ChatGPT, SRLbot, or SRL plus emotional-support bot; pre/post/follow-up measures.

**Measured:** Scaffolded bots improved performance-phase SRL relative to ChatGPT; emotional support also improved enjoyment/boredom. All groups improved knowledge/transfer initially; only ChatGPT showed a significant within-group follow-up decline.

**Inference/limits — C:** A significant decline in one group and nonsignificant decline in others is not itself a significant between-group retention difference. Follow-up interval, aid restrictions, exact learning contrasts, and attrition were not verified. Treat affect/SRL findings as promising, not proof of superior durable learning. No peer review certified on the record.

## AI26-BAR — Delayed retention risk with substantial attrition

**Source:** André Barcaui (2025), *ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch: Evidence from a randomized controlled trial on knowledge retention*, Social Sciences & Humanities Open 12, 102287. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssaho.2025.102287); [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590291125010186).

**Design/access:** Publisher-indexed abstract and follow-up-count passage verified; complete methods unavailable. Randomized study of 120 undergraduates learning AI concepts, unrestricted ChatGPT study aid versus traditional study. Eighty-five completed the surprise 45-day retention test (70.8%).

**Measured:** Retention means 57.5% versus 68.5%, favoring traditional study; reported d=0.68, p=.002.

**Inference/limits — C/B conditional:** Attrition and unavailable full-method checks reduce confidence in transportability. Do not call 120 the analyzed retention sample or infer permanent impairment, broad cognitive damage, or failure of all AI tutoring. Retention depends on the initial learning activity and follow-up measurement; verify differential attrition and assessment conditions before relying on the magnitude.

## AI26-UNE — AI literacy scope: authoritative framework, not treatment evidence

**Source:** UNESCO (2024), *AI competency framework for students*. [Primary overview](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.54675/JKJB9835).

**Design/access:** Official overview and publication metadata inspected. Normative curriculum framework; no experimental sample, comparator, or causal effect.

**Content:** Twelve competency blocks organize human-centred mindset, ethics, techniques/applications, and system design across understand/apply/create progression.

**Inference/limits — guidance:** Use to check breadth and adapt expectations to level. It does not validate a particular lesson, rubric, causal mechanism, or employment forecast. The competency tasks in the companion chapter are this skill's original operationalization, not UNESCO-prescribed assessments. Policy/privacy/legal details still require local current verification.
