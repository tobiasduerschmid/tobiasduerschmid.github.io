## LR-CS-01 — PromptTutor

**Review [14].** Zhang, Y., Ouh, E. L., Ho, C. J. A., Lo, S. L., Tan, K. W., & Lin, F. (2025). *PromptTutor: Effects of an LLM-based chatbot on learning outcomes and motivation in flipped classrooms*. ITiCSE 2025, Vol. 1, 445–451. DOI: 10.1145/3724363.3729095.

**Access:** University publication record and abstract; linked download failed. **Design/outcomes:** Controlled crossover experiment with 50 undergraduate CS students comparing reflective, scaffolded preclass chatbot feedback with a traditional flipped classroom. Authors report statistically significant quiz and motivation benefits.

**Calibration:** C for promising quiz/motivation outcomes in this setting; assessment assistance policy, effect size, carryover checks, and delayed retention unverified. Do not classify the quizzes as confirmed independent learning or infer transferable benefit from this abstract.

**Implication:** Pilot preclass attempt → targeted tutor feedback → revised explanation → question brought to class. Use a separate independent application item to decide whether preparation improved understanding.

**Primary source:** https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/sis_research/10263/

## LR-CS-02 — Learning by teaching a ChatGPT agent

**Review [17].** Chen, A., Wei, Y., Le, H., & Zhang, Y. (2026; online 2025). *Learning by teaching with ChatGPT: The effect of teachable ChatGPT agent on programming education*. British Journal of Educational Technology, 57(1), 163–184. DOI: 10.1111/bjet.70001.

**Access:** Publisher abstract and publication metadata; December 2024 author preprint methods/results §§3–4. Final article methods were unavailable. **Design/outcomes:** Forty-one university students with C++ background; 20 taught GPT-4, 21 used videos and individual programming on backtracking/eight queens. Authors report higher immediate knowledge, pseudocode clarity/readability, and self-regulation ratings; pseudocode correctness did not significantly differ.

**Calibration:** C for these short-term outcomes. Random assignment is not explicit in inspected methods. Preprint reporting inconsistencies preclude confident reuse of its effect sizes; no delayed transfer demonstrated.

**Implication:** Have learners explain decisions to a questioning agent, then independently solve a variation. Include deliberate debugging practice; explanation quality does not establish code correctness.

**Primary sources:** https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/bjet.70001 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2412.15226v1

## LR-CS-03 — Fast and Forgettable

**Review [19].** Gardella, N., Prather, J., Leinonen, J., Denny, P., Pettit, R., & Riggs, S. L. (2026). *Fast and Forgettable: A Controlled Study of Novices’ Performance, Learning, Workload, and Emotion in AI-Assisted and Human Pair Programming Paradigms*. ICER 2026, Vol. 1. DOI: 10.1145/3765964.3811664.

**Access:** Official conference record; author manuscript §§3–4, Tables 1–2. **Design/outcomes:** Counterbalanced within-person comparison, 22 participants, human pairing versus GitHub Copilot on Python tasks; individual unassisted repeat one week later. Initial AI-condition performance increased 14.09/100 (95% CI 6.46–22.89). Absolute retest difference was −4.79 (CI −16.86–7.46; adjusted p=.529). The greater AI-condition performance drop had adjusted p=.054.

**Calibration:** C for task productivity; D for concluding lower retained skill. Wide intervals establish neither equivalence nor harm. Repeated tasks do not test novel transfer.

**Implication:** Record assisted performance and later independent performance separately; a larger drop from a higher assisted starting score does not itself establish worse learning.

**Primary sources:** https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18538v1 ; https://icer2026.acm.org/details/icer-2026-papers/26/Fast-and-Forgettable-A-Controlled-Study-of-Novices-Performance-Learning-Workload-

## LR-CS-04 — GenAI, visualization, and human support in CS2

**Review [20].** Yong, Q., Nacenta, M. A., & Estey, A. (2026). *Characterization and Effects of CS2 Learning with GenAI, Visualization, and Human Support*. ICER 2026, Vol. 1. DOI: 10.1145/3765964.3811663.

**Access:** ACM identity and official program; author manuscript §§4–5. Published author order differs from the early manuscript. **Design/outcomes:** Preregistered, counterbalanced within-person study of 12 CS2 volunteers; 35 of 36 sessions completed. Immediate quizzes prohibited video/support access. Means were 5.6/10 for live tutoring, 5.4 for visualization, 4.4 for general-purpose Copilot. The model's posterior probability that tutoring exceeded GenAI was 97.3%; visualization exceeded GenAI at 96.1%.

**Calibration:** C for immediate conceptual performance in this small setting. These are Bayesian probabilities, not frequentist p-values or general superiority probabilities. No delayed test; one course and specific supports.

**Implication:** Require unaided tracing, variation, complexity, edge-case and comparison questions after supported coding. Choose support using demonstrated understanding, rather than confidence alone.

**Primary sources:** https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02933v1 ; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3765964.3811663 ; https://icer2026.acm.org/track/icer-2026-papers

## LR-CS-05 — AI, human pairing, and individual programming

**Review [24].** Fan, G., Liu, D., Zhang, R., & Pan, L. (2025). *The impact of AI-assisted pair programming on student motivation, programming anxiety, collaborative learning, and programming performance: a comparative study with traditional pair programming and individual approaches*. International Journal of STEM Education, 12, 16. DOI: 10.1186/s40594-025-00537-3.

**Access:** Publisher methods and results, including performance measures and RQ1–3. **Design/outcomes:** Six intact Java web-development classes over 2023–2024; N=234; class-level allocation to AI, human pairing, or individual work. AI groups reported less anxiety and higher motivation, and earned higher assignment/code scores than individual programmers. Human pairs reported stronger collaboration/social presence.

**Calibration:** C for these conditional outcomes; no verified delayed independent learning. Model/cohort and few-class limitations remain. AI-versus-human performance reporting conflicts across abstract, RQ1 and RQ3; do not assert a settled advantage.

**Implication:** Offer bounded AI help for blocking frustration while retaining peer explanation. Evaluate collaboration, assisted artifacts, and individual understanding separately.

**Primary source:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40594-025-00537-3

## LR-CS-06 — Database educational agent

**Review [26].** Li, X., Liu, Z., Jiang, S., Chen, J., & Chen, W. (2026). *The impact of an LLM-based educational agent on learning achievement, cognitive dynamics, and student perceptions in computer science education*. International Journal of STEM Education, 13, 51. DOI: 10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y.

**Access:** Publisher methods and results, especially research procedure and performance comparison. **Design/outcomes:** Four-week quasi-experiment; 313 sophomore database students; three agent classes (246) and one control (67), taught by different instructors. DeepSeek-based DBagent supported two practical tasks. Reported practical scores favored two agent classes, while one underperformed control. Endpoints were task outputs including submitted database design and code, without a separate independent learning test.

**Calibration:** C for heterogeneous assisted task outcomes; D for established durable learning gains. One control class, instructor differences, and extra AI training confound attribution. Nonsignificant baseline tests do not prove equivalence.

**Implication:** Pilot database agents with independent SQL/design checks and class-specific results; inspect interaction quality without treating more chat activity as more learning.

**Primary source:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40594-026-00641-y
