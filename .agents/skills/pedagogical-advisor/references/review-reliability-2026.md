## LR-R-01 — Performance gains need an independent outcome (LR[7])

**Bassner et al. (2026; online 2025). Less stress, better scores, same learning: The dissociation of performance and learning in AI-supported programming education. Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 10, 100537.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100537); [TUM primary record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/less-stress-better-scores-same-learning-the-dissociation-of-perfo/); [research team's account](https://www.edtech.tum.de/the-dissociation-of-performance-and-learning-in-ai-supported-programming-education/).

- **Checked:** institutional abstract and research-team report; publisher full text returned 403. Randomized CS1 study, 275 students, 90-minute concurrency exercise: hint-oriented Iris, unrestricted ChatGPT, or web resources without AI.
- **Result:** AI groups earned higher assisted exercise scores; no detected conceptual-learning/code-comprehension advantage. The team reports reduced frustration and motivational advantages for Iris.
- **Judgment:** B for bounded performance findings; C for learning interpretation pending full methods. Allocation details, missingness, effect intervals, and delayed outcomes were not verified. The title does not establish equivalent learning or durable harmlessness. Claims about a “comfort trap” are interpretations, not a diagnosis of students.

## LR-R-02 — Better help behavior is not sufficient learning evidence (LR[32])

**Aleven et al. (2016). Help Helps, But Only So Much: Research on Help Seeking with Intelligent Tutoring Systems. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 26, 205–223.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1); [author manuscript](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf).

- **Checked:** 22-page author manuscript, targeted PDF pp. 3–8 and 13–16. Research retrospective and synthesis, chiefly geometry tutoring, not a new GenAI trial.
- **Result:** Help Tutor feedback improved deliberate hint use, including after support ended, but the highlighted classroom comparisons did not establish better independent domain learning. Raw hint-use correlations confound help effects with learner difficulty.
- **Judgment/use:** B for this bounded historical evidence; C for GenAI adaptation. Address avoidance and answer-seeking, supply explanations when prerequisites are missing, and evaluate subsequent capability. Full-step help can serve as a worked example when explained. The authors distinguish process/domain outcomes, current/future learning, same/new environment, and same/different domain; transfer across these boundaries must be observed.

## LR-R-03 — A broad average cannot predict a local tutor's effect (LR[37])

**Boolzen et al. (2026). Evidence of impact and interpretational limits of generative AI in STEM education: a systematic review and meta-analysis on cognitive learning outcomes. Artificial Intelligence Review.** [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-026-11665-9); [accepted manuscript](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10462-026-11665-9_reference.pdf).

- **Checked:** publisher abstract, accepted-manuscript results §3.2.3 (PDF pp. 26–27) and sensitivity Table E7 (p. 82). Published online 25 August; search updated 7 May 2026. Review: 85 eligible studies; quantitative subset: 49 studies, 59 effects.
- **Result:** conventional pooled g=0.839, 95% CI [0.53, 1.15], with I²=96.32%. Publication-bias model: posterior mean 0.076, 95% credible interval [−0.37, 0.79]. These intervals answer different statistical questions.
- **Judgment:** B for caution about heterogeneous synthesis; D for a universal benefit or no-effect claim. Bias correction depends on modeling assumptions. Moderator associations do not randomize cognitive activity. The accepted manuscript contains reporting discrepancies; do not copy a single moderator ranking. Overlapping reviews and primary trials are not independent replications. No individual-course effect is established by these averages.

## LR-R-04 — Exclude a retracted estimate (LR[40]; integrity notice)

**Wang & Fan (2026). Retraction Note: The effect of ChatGPT on students’ learning performance, learning perception, and higher-order thinking: insights from a meta-analysis. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 13, 528.** [Publisher notice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-026-07310-z), DOI `10.1057/s41599-026-07310-z`.

The publisher retracted the 2025 article DOI `10.1057/s41599-025-04787-y` on 22 April 2026 over discrepancies undermining confidence in its analysis and conclusions; the notice was updated 2 July. The current notice was read directly. **Exclude that article's effect estimates** from recommendations or pooled support. Retain this entry only to identify and correct citations. A retraction does not discredit unrelated studies. This is not an additional learning study and receives no efficacy grade.

## Application and source checks

Use `procedures/20_ai-learning-support-and-pilots.md` to turn these distinctions into a help plan or local evaluation. Its complete sequences and examples are original designs, not interventions established by the cited papers.

Before using an imported review to change teaching advice:

1. Match DOI/title and distinguish report, participant cohort, synthesis, poster, and notice. Record overlap rather than adding sample sizes.
2. Compare access and versions. A newer secondary summary does not override an older full-method appraisal merely by being newer.
3. Check the publication status of consequential claims, plus any correction affecting the exact result used.
4. For a numerical recommendation, inspect the actual outcome, comparator, uncertainty, and assistance at evaluation. A later assessment using the same questions is narrower than fresh-task transfer.
5. Reuse a design idea with its rationale and limitations, then measure the capability it is intended to improve. Do not convert a review's count of positive reports into a student's probability of benefit.
