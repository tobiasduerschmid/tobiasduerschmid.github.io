# Expected Behaviors Across Evaluation Cases

A competent response should:

1. infer the task mode and use no more than three high-yield questions;
2. state assumptions when proceeding without complete context;
3. begin from target capability and evidence, not a favorite technique;
4. distinguish diagnosis from recommendation;
5. connect recommendations to a plausible learning mechanism;
6. calibrate evidence strength and state conditions/limitations;
7. combine explicit explanation/modeling with student cognitive work as appropriate;
8. consider prior knowledge and expertise;
9. build practice, feedback, and later use—not a one-off activity;
10. include assessment validity, reliability/precision, fairness, and stakes where relevant;
11. consider student and instructor workload;
12. reduce irrelevant access barriers without changing the target construct;
13. treat motivation as value × expectancy × environment rather than a trait defect;
14. structure collaboration and individual accountability;
15. make GenAI rules phase-specific and preserve learning;
16. reject AI-detector scores as proof;
17. avoid learning styles, universal attention-span rules, minimal-guidance dogma, and effect-size recipes;
18. provide a usable artifact—plan, table, prompt, rubric, blueprint, or revision—not advice alone;
19. identify what to evaluate locally;
20. preserve professor agency by offering tradeoffs when more than one defensible design exists.

## Version 2 extensions

See `evals/ai-and-materials-cases.jsonl` for 16 additional prompts. Require actual repairs, complete student tasks and keys, outcome-specific evidence, source access/version honesty, truthful inspection, rescue from unproductive tutor questioning, fair independent assessment, and author/framework provenance. Do not equate structural lint with validity or simulations with student evidence. The corpus is a regression resource; only explicitly recorded cases have been run.
