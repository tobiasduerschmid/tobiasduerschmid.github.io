# Course Design Quality Rubric

Score each dimension 0–4. Use evidence from the actual course map, tasks, and communications—not stated intentions alone.

| Dimension | 0 — Absent/harmful | 1 — Weak | 2 — Developing | 3 — Strong | 4 — Exemplary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose and outcomes | Topic coverage only; no defensible outcomes | Outcomes are vague or numerous | Some observable outcomes but uneven priority | 4–8 consequential, observable outcomes drive design | Outcomes include context, standard, transfer, and explicit non-goals |
| Learner/prerequisite analysis | Assumes a uniform blank slate | General demographic description only | Some prerequisites/misconceptions identified | Diagnostics and supports address variation | Evidence-informed learner model is updated and linked to differentiated decisions |
| Learning progression | Textbook/order-of-content sequence only | Activities accumulate without development | Partial progression for some outcomes | Components, integration, independence, and transfer are sequenced | Empirically plausible progression with adaptive branching and fading |
| Alignment | Outcomes, teaching, assessment, grading conflict | Major gaps or one-and-done outcomes | Basic alignment but weak practice or criteria | Outcomes, practice, feedback, assessment, and grading align | Alignment is audited at course and module levels, including AI and access conditions |
| Practice architecture | Mostly exposure and massed review | Limited practice; high stakes first | Practice exists but is episodic | Retrieval, spacing, variation, and cumulative integration are mapped | Practice dosage and timing are adjusted from learner evidence |
| Teaching/learning methods | Technique-first or passive by default | Occasional activity without cognitive rationale | Mixed methods with uneven guidance | Clear explanation/modeling plus target-aligned guided and active learning | Methods adapt to expertise and make disciplinary thinking visible |
| Assessment system | Grades only; weak validity | Overreliance on one format or memory | Multiple tasks but incomplete sampling/feedback | Diagnostic, formative, summative, integrative, and transfer evidence | Coordinated assessment system with claim–evidence logic and assurance proportional to stakes |
| Feedback and self-regulation | Feedback arrives after learning ends | Comments without required use | Some revision or reflection | Timely, prioritized feedback is used in later attempts; metacognition taught | Students increasingly generate, seek, evaluate, and transfer feedback independently |
| Motivation, climate, belonging | Threatening, opaque, or exclusionary | Relies on exhortation/rewards | Some relevance and support | Value, challenge, autonomy-with-structure, belonging, and help pathways are designed | Climate evidence is monitored and design responds to subgroup experience |
| Accessibility and fairness | Known barriers ignored | Accommodation is reactive only | Basic accessibility and options | UDL-informed access while preserving constructs; transparent participation routes | Accessibility, language, cost, technology, and bias are tested and iteratively improved |
| GenAI coherence | Ambiguous or detector-dependent | Blanket ban/permission unrelated to outcomes | Some assignment rules | Phase-specific rules, verification, disclosure, process and unaided evidence | AI capability development and learning assurance form a coherent course-wide progression |
| Workload and feasibility | Impossible or unknown | Depends on hidden overtime | Rough estimates only | Student and staff workload budgeted; logistics and fallbacks planned | Piloted workload and sustainable operational model with explicit substitutions |
| Evaluation and improvement | Student ratings as sole evidence | Informal impressions | Several data sources but no decision rules | Outcome, implementation, access, and workload evidence drive revision | Course operates as a documented improvement cycle with cautious causal inference |

## Interpretation
- **44–52:** coherent, evidence-informed system; refine local weak points.
- **33–43:** viable design with several material gaps; prioritize dimensions scoring 0–2.
- **22–32:** fragmented course; structural redesign is preferable to isolated techniques.
- **0–21:** learning claims are not defensible; rebuild from outcomes and evidence.

Do not average away a critical failure. A score of 0 in assessment validity, accessibility, or feasibility requires action regardless of total.
