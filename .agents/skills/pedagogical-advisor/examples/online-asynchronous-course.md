# Example — Online Asynchronous Course

## Context

**Course:** Research Methods for Working Professionals, 8 weeks, 45 learners\
**Problem:** Previous version consisted of long videos, discussion-board posts, and a final proposal. Completion was acceptable, but proposals contained weak questions, mismatched methods, and unsupported causal claims.

## Course architecture

Each week follows a predictable learning cycle:

1. **Orient (5 min):** driving decision and relevance.
2. **Retrieve (8 min):** cumulative questions before new material.
3. **Learn (25–40 min):** short annotated readings and 6–10 minute video segments.
4. **Model (15 min):** worked example with expert think-aloud.
5. **Practice (25–45 min):** guided case or data task.
6. **Interact (20–30 min):** structured comparison, not generic posting.
7. **Feedback/revise (15–30 min):** automated correctness plus instructor/peer feedback.
8. **Integrate (10 min):** update a living research-design canvas.

## Weekly progression

| Week | Target decision | Major evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | distinguish problem, question, and claim | critique weak questions |
| 2 | identify constructs and operationalizations | construct map |
| 3 | choose design for descriptive/causal aims | contrasting-case analysis |
| 4 | evaluate sampling and bias | sampling diagnosis |
| 5 | interpret uncertainty and effect | data commentary |
| 6 | address ethics and feasibility | ethics memo |
| 7 | integrate design choices | proposal draft |
| 8 | defend and revise a design | proposal + oral defense |

## Interaction design

Instead of “post your thoughts and reply to two peers,” learners:
- independently choose a method for the same case;
- reveal choices after submission;
- compare one contrasting peer design using a decision protocol;
- jointly identify the assumption that explains the difference;
- revise their own decision.

Individual preparation and a synthesizing revision preserve accountability.

## Presence and feedback

Instructor presence consists of:
- a weekly preview tied to learner work;
- a short “patterns I saw” synthesis;
- response within a stated window for high-level questions;
- two live optional clinics recorded with accessible notes;
- targeted feedback on proposal milestones.

Video quantity is not used as a proxy for presence.

## Assessment

- cumulative retrieval quizzes: 10%;
- six decision memos: 24%;
- peer/self-assessment quality: 6%;
- staged proposal: 40%;
- individual oral defense with a changed scenario: 20%.

The oral defense samples transfer and independent judgment rather than policing authorship alone.

## GenAI policy

AI is allowed to brainstorm alternative research questions after the learner submits two of their own, and to critique clarity after a method is selected. Learners must:
- preserve their initial attempt;
- identify one AI suggestion accepted and one rejected;
- verify methodological claims against assigned sources;
- never upload employer-confidential data;
- disclose tool/version and substantive use.

## Access and workload

- Materials are downloadable and mobile-readable.
- Audio has transcript; video has captions and a text-equivalent path.
- Deadlines use a 72-hour completion window.
- Tasks use public or synthetic data.
- Expected weekly workload is 5–7 hours, with a published breakdown.
- The final project replaces, rather than adds to, weekly work because the canvas accumulates into the proposal.

## Evaluation

Use a common proposal rubric across cohorts, a novel-method-choice task, course analytics interpreted cautiously, time-on-task reports, completion, and qualitative interviews about barriers. Discussion-post counts are not treated as learning outcomes.
