# Example — Professional Skills Course: Clinical/Technical Judgment

## Context

**Course:** Incident Response and Root-Cause Analysis for early-career engineers\
**Format:** six 3-hour workshops plus field practice\
**Problem:** Learners know procedures but fixate on the first plausible cause, communicate poorly under uncertainty, and fail to transfer checklists to messy incidents.

## Capability model

Competent performance includes:
- retrieving safety-critical procedures;
- noticing diagnostic cues;
- generating and ranking hypotheses;
- seeking disconfirming evidence;
- coordinating roles and handoffs;
- documenting uncertainty;
- deciding when to escalate;
- learning from near misses without blame.

## Instructional progression

1. **Tell–show–do–review** for critical procedures.
2. **Worked incident:** instructor models cue selection, hypothesis revision, and communication.
3. **Completion case:** learners fill missing decisions and justify them.
4. **Contrast cases:** similar symptoms, different causes; identify discriminating cues.
5. **Team simulation:** timed incident with role rotation and observable checkpoints.
6. **After-action review:** compare intended process, actual process, outcome, and system conditions.
7. **Field transfer:** learner documents a real or synthetic near miss and proposes a testable improvement.

## Simulation design

The simulation does not reward speed alone. The scoring model includes:
- safety and escalation;
- evidence collection;
- quality of hypothesis testing;
- coordination and closed-loop communication;
- documentation;
- adaptation when new evidence appears.

A technically correct outcome reached through unsafe reasoning does not receive a high score.

## Feedback

- Immediate feedback is limited during the simulation to safety-critical intervention.
- The replay uses an event timeline and selected video/log evidence.
- Learners first self-assess, then peers identify decision points, then the facilitator adds expert comparison.
- The team reruns the critical segment with one changed variable.
- Individual learners complete a transfer plan and retrieve the procedure one week later.

## Assessment system

| Evidence | Use |
|---|---|
| procedure retrieval checks | safety prerequisite |
| observed simulation rubric | integrated team performance |
| individual changed-case diagnosis | independent judgment |
| incident report | evidence and communication |
| field improvement proposal | authentic transfer |

## Motivation and climate

Cases are consequential and realistic. Error is treated as information about the system, while safety violations remain accountable. Psychological safety is not the absence of standards; it permits speaking up, uncertainty, and challenge before harm occurs.

## GenAI use

AI may generate alternative hypotheses during debrief only after the team records its own. Learners must evaluate suggestions against logs and identify hallucinated or unsafe advice. AI is prohibited during the initial safety-critical simulation because rapid unaided procedure access is an explicit target.
