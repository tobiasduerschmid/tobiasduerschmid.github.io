# Example — Large Lecture Redesign Without Abandoning Lecturing

## Context

**Course:** Introductory Psychology, 520 students, 75-minute meetings\
**Constraint:** Fixed lecture theatre, two instructors, eight TAs, no required attendance\
**Problem:** Students report enjoying lectures but perform poorly on application and confuse familiarity with mastery.

## Redesign principle

The response is not “stop explaining.” The response is to make explanation selective and embed it in a cycle of retrieval, prediction, peer reasoning, evidence, and cumulative practice.

## Standard 75-minute architecture

| Time | Activity | Student cognitive work | Instructor evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0–6 | 3 cumulative retrieval prompts | recall and discriminate | response distribution |
| 6–18 | explanation around one causal model | organize and connect | sampled paraphrase |
| 18–27 | prediction about a study result | commit and explain | first vote |
| 27–34 | peer instruction | compare reasoning | revote/change |
| 34–41 | debrief and methodological critique | reconcile evidence | questions/response |
| 41–51 | worked example of data interpretation | follow expert decisions | completion prompt |
| 51–63 | small-team case | apply and justify | submitted rationale |
| 63–69 | hinge question | diagnose misconception | all-student response |
| 69–75 | retrieval, synthesis, next-step plan | generate summary | exit sample |

## Example: operant conditioning

Rather than list reinforcement schedules, students compare four cases with similar surface features. They must identify:
- the behavior;
- the consequence;
- whether frequency increases or decreases;
- whether a stimulus is added or removed;
- which schedule best predicts persistence.

The cases are interleaved because the target is strategy selection, not recitation. Distractors map to common confusions about “positive” meaning good and “negative” meaning bad.

## Response system without participation theater

- Students first answer individually.
- Correctness is not graded; participation earns a small course contribution with alternative routes.
- Peer discussion is used only when the distribution indicates productive disagreement.
- The instructor asks for reasoning before revealing the answer.
- If 80%+ are correct for the right reason, the class advances to a transfer item.
- If responses split, peers discuss.
- If most are wrong, the instructor does not ask novices to teach one another; a new representation or worked example is provided first.

## Outside-class structure

- Weekly cumulative low-stakes quiz, 10 questions, with two delayed items from earlier units.
- Feedback explains why distractors are attractive.
- A second attempt is available after an error-classification prompt.
- Reading guides ask students to retrieve and predict, not highlight chapters.
- Practice tests mirror the blueprint but not the exact items.

## TA system

TAs receive:
- question rationales and misconception maps;
- a facilitation protocol;
- a weekly calibration meeting using sample student explanations;
- a dashboard of common errors;
- escalation guidance for access and student-support issues.

## Accessibility and equity

- Captions/transcripts and accessible slide documents are released.
- Essential diagrams use labels/patterns in addition to color.
- all-student response reduces reliance on volunteered speech.
- students can earn preparation credit through equivalent online retrieval when attendance is impossible.
- no device? paper response cards and post-class submission are available.

## Evaluation

Do not rely on enjoyment ratings alone. Use common exam items, transfer questions, failure rates, response patterns, quiz use, subgroup experience, and student time. Also measure the “feeling of learning” because active learning may feel less fluent even when performance improves.
