# Example — GenAI-Resilient Assignment Redesign

## Original assignment

> Write a 2,500-word report explaining three causes of urban heat islands and proposing interventions. Use eight sources.

### Why it is weak in the AI era

A competent model can generate a fluent report, sources may be fabricated or weak, and the final text provides little evidence that the student can evaluate local data, make tradeoffs, or defend causal reasoning. Trying to make the prompt obscure would not solve the inference problem.

## Redesigned assignment: Local Heat-Risk Decision Brief

### Learning targets
Students can:
1. interpret local spatial and temperature data;
2. distinguish causal mechanism from correlation;
3. compare interventions under equity, cost, feasibility, and uncertainty constraints;
4. verify claims and communicate a defensible recommendation;
5. use GenAI as a fallible analytic partner without surrendering judgment.

### Scenario
A local authority has funds for one pilot intervention in a specified neighborhood. Students receive a shared data pack plus choose one additional public data source. They must recommend, reject, or conditionally support an intervention for a named decision audience.

### Stages and evidence

| Stage | Student-generated evidence | AI condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1. In-class data interpretation | individual annotated map and three claims | prohibited |
| 2. Causal model | hand-drawn/structured mechanism model + uncertainty | prohibited until submitted |
| 3. Intervention shortlist | student criteria and two options | AI permitted to generate one additional option |
| 4. Verification lab | claim-source table, calculation checks, bias/error log | AI required as object of critique |
| 5. Draft decision brief | recommendation with tradeoffs | AI permitted for counterargument and clarity, disclosed |
| 6. Peer review and revision | rubric-based feedback-use memo | AI optional, not as peer substitute |
| 7. Oral defense | five-minute defense using a changed budget or stakeholder constraint | prohibited |

### Deliverables
- 1-page causal model;
- evidence table with provenance and confidence;
- 1,500-word decision brief;
- AI interaction appendix limited to substantive uses;
- 300-word reflection on accepted/rejected AI suggestions;
- individual oral defense.

### Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Strong evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Data interpretation | 20% | correct patterns, uncertainty, and limits |
| Causal reasoning | 20% | mechanism, alternatives, and disconfirming evidence |
| Decision quality | 20% | criteria, tradeoffs, feasibility, and equity |
| Evidence integrity | 15% | credible sources, traceable claims, verified calculations |
| Evaluative judgment about AI | 10% | detects limitations, justifies accept/reject decisions |
| Communication | 10% | audience-appropriate, precise, accessible |
| Process/revision | 5% | feedback and evidence visibly improve the work |

### Verification protocol
For every substantive AI-assisted claim:
1. classify it as factual, inferential, computational, or advisory;
2. locate an independent source or reproduce the calculation;
3. record whether it was confirmed, modified, or rejected;
4. state remaining uncertainty;
5. accept personal responsibility for inclusion.

### Access and privacy
- The course provides a supported tool and a no-cost non-AI pathway.
- Only public/synthetic data may be uploaded.
- Students may redact prompts that contain personal reflection while still describing the function used.
- Tool failure does not affect grading; evaluative judgment, not model quality, is assessed.

### Why this design is resilient

The assignment does not depend on a model being unable to write. It gathers multiple forms of evidence across time, includes an initial unaided attempt, assesses local data and decision tradeoffs, requires verification, and samples independent transfer in an oral defense. AI can improve, but it cannot erase the need for the student's defensible judgment.
