# Intake and Diagnosis

## High-yield intake fields

Collect what is available:

### Context
- institution and level;
- discipline/topic;
- course role and prerequisites;
- class size;
- modality;
- term/session length;
- required standards.

### Learners
- prior knowledge;
- variability;
- common misconceptions;
- language and accessibility;
- motivation and belonging;
- time and technology access.

### Current design
- outcomes;
- activities;
- assessments;
- grading;
- resources;
- staff;
- GenAI policy;
- what works and what fails.

### Request
- brainstorm, critique, generate, improve, or complete;
- desired format;
- deadline;
- constraints;
- non-negotiables.

## Three-question rule

Ask only questions with high decision value. Examples:
1. “What should students be able to do independently by the end?”
2. “What evidence currently suggests they are not learning it?”
3. “Which constraint is least negotiable: contact time, marking time, content coverage, or assessment format?”

If the user provides artifacts, analyze them before asking questions.

## Diagnostic matrix

For a reported problem, map evidence:

| Observation | Possible explanation | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Students fail exam but complete homework | assistance, blocked practice, assessment mismatch, cramming | unaided delayed tasks, homework process, blueprint |
| Students are silent | low prior knowledge, threat, unclear question, no wait time | anonymous responses, written prethink, interviews |
| Students like lecture but retain little | fluency illusion, passive exposure | delayed retrieval, transfer |
| Group products are strong but individuals weak | division of labor, unequal participation | individual quiz/oral check |
| AI-assisted work is polished but oral defense weak | product not evidence of capability | process logs, unaided sample |
| High failure concentrated by group | prerequisite/opportunity/climate/access differences | disaggregated diagnostic and participation data |

## Root-cause protocol

1. Describe the performance gap without blaming learners.
2. Identify the target capability.
3. Check whether it was taught and practiced.
4. Check whether assessment measured it.
5. Check prerequisite and load.
6. Check feedback and time to use it.
7. Check motivation, climate, and access.
8. Check workload and logistics.
9. Check tool/AI support.
10. Rank hypotheses by evidence and reversibility.

## Output

Provide:
- concise diagnosis;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- immediate low-risk move;
- deeper design options;
- data to collect.
