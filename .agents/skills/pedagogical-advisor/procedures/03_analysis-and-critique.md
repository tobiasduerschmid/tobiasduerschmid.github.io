# Analysis and Critique Protocol

## Critique stance

Critique the design, not the professor. Preserve effective features. Distinguish:
- fatal validity problems;
- high-impact learning problems;
- implementation risks;
- refinements.

## Evidence extraction

From the artifact, identify:
- stated outcomes;
- implied outcomes;
- learner tasks;
- instructor tasks;
- assessment evidence;
- criteria and grades;
- sequence and timing;
- feedback;
- assumptions;
- access and AI rules.

## Alignment audit

For each outcome:
- Where is it taught?
- Where is it practiced?
- What feedback occurs?
- Where is it assessed?
- Does grading represent it?
- Is evidence individual or supported?
- Is transfer required?
- Is AI use compatible?

## Critique lenses

Apply:
1. learning mechanism;
2. prior knowledge;
3. load;
4. practice;
5. feedback;
6. motivation;
7. inclusion;
8. assessment inference;
9. workload;
10. GenAI;
11. sustainability;
12. evaluation.

## Priority scale

- **P0 — invalid/unsafe:** assessment cannot support the claimed inference; serious access, ethics, or policy issue.
- **P1 — high impact:** likely to prevent important learning.
- **P2 — substantial:** meaningful improvement.
- **P3 — refinement:** clarity, polish, or optional enhancement.

Limit the top recommendations to what can be acted on.

## Critique output

1. Overall judgment
2. Strengths to preserve
3. P0/P1 issues
4. P2/P3 issues
5. Concrete revision
6. Rationale and evidence level
7. Alternative under constraints
8. Quality-gate status

Quote or point to specific elements rather than giving generic advice.

## Anti-patterns

Do not:
- equate preference with quality;
- recommend active learning without designing the activity;
- demand more content;
- add assessments without removing burden;
- rewrite the professor’s disciplinary goals without cause;
- overclaim certainty;
- treat all deviations from a template as errors.
