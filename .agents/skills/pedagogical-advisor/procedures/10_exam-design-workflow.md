# Exam Design Workflow

## Step 1: Define exam purpose

- diagnostic, practice, midterm, final, certification;
- stakes and consequences;
- closed/open resource;
- supervised/take-home;
- independent/tool-mediated.

## Step 2: Blueprint

Allocate:
- outcomes;
- content;
- cognitive demand;
- item formats;
- marks;
- time;
- difficulty;
- transfer;
- cumulative content.

Do not draft items first.

## Step 3: Item specifications

For each item:
- outcome;
- evidence;
- format;
- prompt;
- key/rubric;
- common errors/distractors;
- difficulty;
- time;
- accessibility;
- AI/security note.

## Step 4: Draft

Follow format-specific guidelines. Avoid tricks, irrelevant reading load, and cueing.

For practice based on an existing exam:

- Inspect the original task, available solution, and corresponding course
  material. Match the action students must perform, not just the topic label.
- Use a different, small example that retains the meaningful decision. Avoid
  prompts that prescribe the solution steps when choosing those steps is the
  skill being assessed.
- Keep the instructor's introductory model. State a necessary simplifying
  assumption and explain its boundary briefly; do not replace the task with
  advanced exceptions.
- For shuffled practice, include all necessary context in each item. If this
  requires a long reading or several files, omit it from the short-practice
  bank or redesign it as one small task within the authorized scope.

Before accepting each item, hide its answer and solve it from the visible
prompt. Trace every factual premise in the key back to either the stem or an
expected course concept. Initial state, transaction scope, function behavior,
and entities introduced only in the answer are missing context. Supply the
minimum necessary facts without revealing the decision, or revise the question.
An explicitly requested example may invent details, but its answer must be
labeled as one possible example. Apply this check across the entire set.

## Step 5: Independent review

Have another knowledgeable person:
- solve;
- time;
- identify ambiguity;
- map to blueprint;
- check accuracy and bias.

## Step 6: Scoring

Prepare:
- key;
- partial credit;
- rubric;
- alternative answers;
- moderation;
- marker training.

## Step 7: Administration

Specify:
- instructions;
- allowed materials;
- accommodations;
- technology fallback;
- integrity;
- question handling;
- extra-time logistics.

## Step 8: Student preparation

Provide:
- outcome scope;
- format;
- practice items;
- standards;
- retrieval plan;
- not exact replicas.

## Step 9: Post-exam review

Analyze:
- item functioning;
- timing;
- reliability/dependability;
- common errors;
- subgroup patterns;
- teaching implications.

Correct flawed items transparently.

## Step 10: Learning after exam

Use:
- corrections;
- wrapper;
- reattempt;
- cumulative return.

Run `rubrics/exam-design-rubric.md`.
