# Evidence update workflow

Use for research requests, contested claims, and decisions that depend on current AI evidence, guidance, or standards. Scale to consequence: a wording repair need not become a literature review.

## 1. Specify the claim

Define population/prior preparation, intervention and version, comparison, measured outcome, assistance conditions, time horizon, setting, and the decision at stake. Distinguish a claim about product quality, learning, experience, safety, workload, or normative responsibility.

## 2. Search a complementary set of sources

Use requested available research connectors; verify important findings in publisher, author, repository, or official institutional records. For computing education, search SIGCSE TS, ITiCSE, ICER, TOCE and relevant CHI, IUI, Koli Calling, Learning @ Scale and FSE work. A venue program proves publication/description, not an inaccessible paper's full methods. For relevant computing-teaching decisions, include SIGCSE TS 2024 and 2025 findings alongside 2026 work and older foundational evidence. Use the published conference year rather than an earlier preprint/indexing year. Record Technical Symposium, Virtual, poster, and adjacent-venue contributions accurately.

Use several angles: intervention/outcome; novice/advanced moderators; delayed unaided transfer; contrary/null findings; fidelity and access; correction/replication. Search current guidance separately from treatment-efficacy research. Include older strong syntheses when the mechanism warrants them. Date filters can miss late-indexed records, so inspect document dates as well.

If a service is unavailable, record the actual limitation and continue through available sources. Do not imply an account/API was accessible, silently treat an index gap as absence of research, or invent results to complete a search log.

## 3. Maintain an auditable source card

Record:

- stable source ID; exact author/title/year/venue; DOI or primary URL;
- publication status and inspected version; publication and access dates;
- access scope: full text, selected sections, abstract, metadata, or inaccessible;
- population, design, allocation unit, comparator, duration, actual analytic N if verified;
- outcome and aid conditions; immediate/delayed/transfer distinction;
- effect and uncertainty only as verified; absolute versus relative changes;
- attrition, contamination, selection, rater dependence, clustered data, multiple tests, and missing limitations relevant to inference;
- editorial claim-specific evidence judgment, practical implication, and next verification needed.

Do not report student-session observations, feedback outputs, or a whole course enrollment as a randomized/analytic student sample. Deduplicate DOI and research lineage; a revised working paper is not a replication. Prefer updated methods without silently mixing revised sample sizes with earlier estimates.

## 4. Reconcile before recommending

Check whether apparently conflicting findings concern the same learners, assistance, comparison, and outcome. Ask whether the model, teacher preparation, prompt, pedagogy, interface, and time budget changed together. Avoid attributing a bundled effect to one ingredient.

Compare confidence intervals where available. Nonsignificance is not equivalence. Significance in one group but not another is not an interaction test. Qualitative patterns and self-reports can identify mechanisms to investigate, not causal learning gains. Generated-content and AI benchmark studies do not certify learner competence.

Do not let an abstract's optimistic phrasing override detailed results. Check corrections, version changes, later replication, and author limitations. Do not make a precise forecast of a local course's gain from a literature effect size.

For supplied literature reviews, map records to existing cards before adding evidence; distinguish a shared cohort, companion report, review, and retraction. Preserve the review’s search cutoff and inspection history separately from this task’s verification. Use the stronger primary appraisal when a secondary summary conflicts. Check the publisher for retractions and corrections before relying on consequential pooled estimates; inspect the affected claim instead of assuming an entire field is invalid. Keep conventional and bias-adjusted estimates, confidence and credible intervals, and descriptive report counts distinct. The applied example is `references/literature-review-crosswalk.md`.

## 5. Translate into action

Give the educator: when it fits, required student action, teacher support, feedback/reattempt, access/time cost, failure mode, and a practical local learning check. Label a combined sequence, mnemonic, rubric, scoring weight, or decision threshold as **original design synthesis** unless an identified source validates it. Preserve consequential knowledge needed to judge AI output.

For decisions about adoption, define what would make the intervention worth keeping and what result would trigger revision. Include a non-AI or supported-access path where relevant. State evidence gaps without stopping useful design work.

## 6. Report coverage truthfully

Provide actual queries/services/date, selection rationale, access limits, and key unresolved claims. Do not invent systematic-review counts or imply exhaustive coverage. Cite primary evidence for claims and policy for policy. Keep copyright-compliant summaries; do not distribute full third-party papers inside the skill.

Only when the user requests skill maintenance, update the appropriate evidence ledger and routes and validate/save the skill. For an ordinary teaching task, deliver the answer/artifact without silently changing the installed skill. Existing focused update: `references/research-coverage-2026.md`.
