# Brainstorming Protocol

Use this when the professor wants ideas, not an immediate final answer.

## Principles

- Diverge before converging.
- Generate alternatives that differ in mechanism, not just theme.
- Keep options tied to learning targets and constraints.
- Avoid presenting a giant list without structure.
- Preserve instructor ownership.

## Session structure

### 1. Frame the design challenge
Complete:
> How might we help [learners] develop [capability] under [constraints], while addressing [risk]?

### 2. Establish design criteria
Choose 4–7:
- learning impact;
- transfer;
- feasibility;
- marking burden;
- inclusivity;
- student motivation;
- authenticity;
- academic integrity;
- GenAI relevance;
- scalability;
- instructor comfort.

### 3. Generate along dimensions
Use prompts:
- change the evidence, not the topic;
- change when feedback occurs;
- change who explains;
- change level of guidance;
- change authenticity;
- change representation;
- change individual/group sequence;
- change AI role;
- change stakes;
- change time distribution;
- change audience;
- change recurrence.

### 4. Produce option cards
For each:
- title;
- core idea;
- student work;
- instructor work;
- learning mechanism;
- evidence;
- burden;
- risk;
- evidence strength.

### 5. Compare
Use a decision matrix, not intuition alone.

### 6. Combine
Hybridize compatible strengths.

### 7. Select next experiment
Choose:
- minimal viable version;
- evidence to collect;
- decision date.

## Brainstorming modes

- **Constraint flip:** assume a constraint changes.
- **Worst idea:** generate a deliberately bad design, then reverse its features.
- **Analogical transfer:** borrow a structure from another discipline.
- **Learner journey:** design from a struggling student’s sequence.
- **Assessment-first:** begin with evidence of learning.
- **Bottleneck-first:** begin with expert decision points.
- **AI role matrix:** no AI / AI tutor / AI collaborator / AI object of critique.
- **Three budgets:** 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 3 weeks.

## Output behavior

Offer 3–5 options, then recommend one only if criteria justify it. State why alternatives may suit different values.
