# Assessment Design Workflow

## Step 1: Purpose and decision

State:
- formative, summative, practice, certification, or program evaluation;
- stakes;
- decision;
- audience.

## Step 2: Claim

Write:
> Successful students can ______ under ______ conditions at ______ standard.

Clarify independent, collaborative, or AI-augmented capability.

## Step 3: Evidence

List observable features:
- decisions;
- reasoning;
- accuracy;
- organization;
- adaptation;
- quality;
- process;
- ethical judgment.

List alternative explanations to rule out.

## Step 4: Task design

Choose a task that elicits evidence. Specify:
- scenario;
- input;
- output;
- constraints;
- resources;
- audience;
- stages;
- authenticity;
- support.

## Step 5: Criteria

Create 3–7 substantive criteria and standards. Use exemplars. Decide analytic, holistic, or mixed scoring.

## Step 6: Learning preparation

Map:
- prerequisite diagnosis;
- models;
- component practice;
- whole-task practice;
- feedback;
- revision;
- transfer.

## Step 7: Assessment process

Specify:
- milestones;
- feedback;
- peer/self-assessment;
- submission;
- identity/process evidence;
- moderation;
- appeals.

## Step 8: Fairness and access

Check:
- construct-irrelevant language/technology;
- resource equity;
- accommodations;
- alternative modes;
- cultural assumptions;
- timing;
- group conditions.

## Step 9: GenAI

State:
- allowed/required/prohibited;
- disclosure;
- verification;
- process artifacts;
- independent evidence;
- privacy.

## Step 10: Burden and feasibility

Estimate student and marker time. Pilot task and rubric.

## Step 11: Validation review

Use the assessment triangle:
- cognition;
- observation;
- interpretation.

Review content representation, scoring dependability, consequences, and fairness.
