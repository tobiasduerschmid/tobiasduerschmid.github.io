# SE Gym avatar art direction

The hero should read as a friendly, dimensional illustration at both the full-page size and the small customizer preview. Its identity comes from a coherent silhouette, expressive features, and selectable personal details. A large option count is useful only when those options compose cleanly.

## Research and decisions

| Primary source | What it establishes | Application to this hero |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Open Peeps, by Pablo Stanley](https://www.openpeeps.com/) | A modular vector library combines separate expressions, hair, clothing, bodies, and poses. | Keep traits independently selectable. Maintain compatible attachment points and silhouettes rather than making isolated presets that cannot mix. |
| [DiceBear Avataaars](https://www.dicebear.com/styles/avataaars/) | A cartoon vector system provides varied hair, clothing, accessories, and facial expressions. | Keep the result distinctly illustrated and expressive; retain stable saved option values while improving their artwork. |
| [DiceBear Adventurer](https://www.dicebear.com/styles/adventurer/) | Bold outlines and a restrained cartoon vocabulary can support expressive faces and varied accessories. | Use consistent outline weight, grouped detail, and clear facial features instead of adding detail indiscriminately. |
| [xz_art: Drawing Stylised Hair](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/6072) | Hair can be developed through silhouette, directional tufts, and selective strands. Rounded planes facing the light carry highlights. | Design the mass first, vary curl and tuft size, follow the growth direction, and reserve narrow highlights for a few rounded locks. |
| [AvionArt: How to Draw Black Hairstyles](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/6010) | Box braids begin in scalp sections, drape from those roots, and narrow toward their ends. Natural coils are built from an overall volume with varied curved edges. | Connect protective styles to their scalp sections, taper the hanging strands, and vary the contour rhythm. Adapt the construction guidance while keeping all traits independent of gender. |
| [Avataaars hijab component](https://github.com/fangpenlin/avataaars/blob/master/src/avatar/top/Hijab.tsx) | The source combines a continuous fabric silhouette, a face opening, masked color, and separate curved highlight and fold-shadow shapes. | Preserve the face aperture and use curved fabric planes with restrained shadows to suggest volume. |
| [Mack et al., Towards Inclusive Avatars (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01880) | Interviews with 18 people with disabilities and related identities found that representation preferences depended on context, safety, and available choices. | Offer optional personal details such as hearing aids and skin variation. Never infer disability, ethnicity, gender, or religious identity from another selected trait. |

The illustration sources are working design precedents and practitioner guidance, not controlled evidence of improved learning. The interview study concerns avatar identity representation; it does not establish that a particular SVG style improves student attainment or represents every student's preferences. These recommendations adapt that evidence to the SE Gym context.

## Visual system

- **One light direction.** Use a broad upper-left key light, a restrained lower-right form shadow, and small highlights where the material warrants them. Skin is soft; hair has grouped sheen; glass and metal receive sharper reflections.
- **Clip light to its surface.** Face lighting must follow every head silhouette and the user's width, height, spread, and vertical tuning. A bright edge outside the jaw reads as disconnected artwork.
- **Natural hair mass.** Preserve a connected crown and temple area. Front tufts should vary in size and direction; rear hair establishes depth. Curly and coiled styles need variation within the overall mass, without a repeated scallop rhythm.
- **Color-preserving hair light.** Blend decorative light planes and sheen toward the selected hair color so black, silver, blond and vivid hair retain their material identity. Keep outline and feature contrast tokens independent of this decorative treatment. Parted crowns should follow the scalp; curl highlights should describe local clumps rather than form one repeating stripe.
- **Expressive eyes.** Catchlights belong inside the visible iris or eye opening. Compact, smiling, and closed-eye variants need their own compatible reflection treatment.
- **Quiet face detail.** Keep the eyes and mouth legible before adding skin texture. Optional birthmarks and vitiligo should follow the face surface and remain independent of skin color and other traits. Skin materials paint beneath opaque facial anatomy. Keep the secondary skin patch away from the smile corner so it does not resemble a tooth.
- **Subtle expressions still read.** Closed-mouth expressions combine a restrained lower-lip plane with the contrast-aware mouth seam. Check the seam on deep skin at thumbnail size; simply making the whole lip darker can make the expression disappear.
- **Materials own their detail.** Costume panels belong to the super-suit. Clothing must not tint exposed neck skin, and cheek shading belongs beneath opaque facial hair. Small chin patches stay on the jaw; mustaches follow the actual upper lip while preserving their complete contours.
- **Accessories remain connected.** Earbud cables join the fitted buds to the selected garment. Measure these attachment points after independent proportion controls have been applied. The Bruin's nose and smile use contrast against the muzzle surface beneath them, separately from the outer fur outline.
- **Equal finish.** Apply the same care to short, long, straight, wavy, curly, coiled, protective, bald, and covered-head styles. Cultural clothing and assistive details should have the same material quality as the superhero costume.
- **User choice.** Hearing aids, birthmarks, and vitiligo patches are optional and excluded from random generation. Body frame, skin tone, facial structure, hair, clothing, and accessories must not be grouped into inferred demographic categories.

## Rendering and verification contract

The existing `data-gym-hero-svg`, `data-hero-slot`, and `data-hero-option` attributes identify the public artwork parts used by the renderer. `data-hero-polish="face"` identifies the portrait light layer; `data-hero-eye-catchlights` identifies the shared eye reflections; `data-hero-skin-details` identifies the surface-clipped optional skin details. `data-hero-eye-surface="left|right"` identifies each eye aperture, `data-hero-eye-cover` the eyepatch cover, `data-hero-eye-strap` its fitted temple straps, and `data-hero-chin-detail` the fitted chin dimple. Tests may locate these parts to measure rendered paint; they should not compare path strings or require a specific number of curves.

`tests/se-gym-hero-art-quality.spec.js` rasterizes the real face-light layer and selected head with their composed transforms, and does the same for shared reflections against the selected eye shape. It checks every registered head and eye option at default and extreme tuning combinations. Both optional skin details are checked across all heads and independently combined head/detail tuning extremes; cheek and head-contour shading are checked across all heads with opposing tuning extremes. A one-pixel edge tolerance handles antialiasing while detecting exterior paint. Separate cases check the new personal details through selection, saving, and reload, and confirm that repeated SVG mounts resolve their own gradients, masks, and clips. These protect visible rendering and interaction contracts without committing to an exact screenshot.

`tests/se-gym-hero-artifacts.spec.js` checks that the complete face follows head adjustments while the neck, ears and lashes remain physically attached. Mouth fitting must preserve the complete smile, including at each mouth-control boundary; clipping away lips is not a valid containment fix. Body accessories must stay in place when the head moves. Full-coverage hair caps must cover the filled scalp at minimized hair proportions. That last check excludes the head's decorative outer ink contour, measures opaque hair paint, and keeps the same small antialiasing tolerance.

Review the artwork at full size and thumbnail size, in light and dark themes, with light skin/dark hair, dark skin/light hair, and dark skin/dark hair. Also review wide and narrow heads, head coverings, glasses, facial hair, and the lifted pose. Verify that each SVG instance resolves its own gradients, clips, and masks. Keep pause and reduced-motion behavior intact.

Both static thumbnails and the live fallback must paint the represented option. A correctly drawn barbell or background cannot stand in for a missing character. The Bruin fallback regression measures paint from the mascot itself, guarding against template pruning that removes the alternate character before it is selected.

Detached and hidden preview templates need real layout before group bounds can be measured. The renderer mounts them synchronously in an invisible measuring host and restores their original parent, sibling position, dimensions and visibility in a `finally` block. A regression compares detached and mounted paint, requires visible artwork, and checks that no measuring host or ownership change remains.

This check cannot certify visual taste, complete representation, every accessory combination, or WCAG conformance. Continue the existing interaction, geometry, color-contrast, persistence, and accessibility suites alongside a human visual review. The current standing barbell pose also cannot express every mobility experience; representation remains an ongoing design responsibility.

Opaque headwear must clear the current eye apertures after both eye and accessory tuning. Recompute the hair envelope after moving the complete hat. The eyepatch must cover its intended eye, keep the other eye unobstructed, and retain connected straps. Nose fitting measures the upper-lip contour beneath the nose, not the elevated corners of a smile.
