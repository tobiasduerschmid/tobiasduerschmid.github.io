# Fact check: How Should I Use AI as a College Student?

Reviewed 15 September 2026. Target: `_posts/2026-03-17-how-should-i-use-ai-as-a-college-student.md`.

## Scope and method

The review covered **all 43 distinct sources cited in the draft at the start of the task**. The supplied `RelatedWork` collection includes research articles, preprints, three books, reviews, reports, and a practitioner essay. The complete substantive texts were read: methods, results, tables, discussion, limitations, and supplied substantive appendices. Books were read across all chapters, not just the passages matching the draft. External supplements not included in the supplied documents were not read. Most image-only figures were reviewed through captions and accompanying results rather than individually inspected; exceptions are noted below. Reference lists and indexes were used for identification and checking; they were not all exhaustively line-read. Reading a review did not include independently retrieving every primary study it cites.

PDF text was extracted in both layout-preserving and reading-order forms. Truncated reading outputs were recovered in smaller sections. Key apparent inconsistencies in the Kosmyna and Gkintoni PDFs were also checked visually against the original pages. No supplied source files were modified. The pedagogical-advisor skill guided the distinction between learning goals, instructional activities, and evidence of learning.

**Version boundary:** This is a review of the supplied versions, with selective publisher/arXiv metadata checks. For example, Huang's supplied manuscript is version 2, dated 18 August 2026, although its first posting was in 2025. Yan's 2025 commentary was supplied as an accepted manuscript uploaded in May 2026. The post retains its original draft date and now states that its research review extends through September 2026. This is not a systematic update of the entire literature or a reproduction of the studies' statistical analyses.

The main message survives: assess what students can do independently, preserve relevant practice, and verify generated help. The corrections concern causal inference, duration of measured outcomes, participant populations, actual instructional designs, and unsupported generalizations.

## Principal corrections

1. **Expertise does not universally increase the AI benefit.** Paradis did not find significant moderation by seniority. Ma and Prather do not establish a causal law that expert developers benefit more. DORA's amplification claim concerns organizational conditions in survey data.
2. **Immediate performance is not durable learning.** Bastani and Shen measured short-term unaided assessments. Neither establishes permanent loss or failure to form neural pathways. Fan's lack of a between-group advantage is not an absence of learning within the AI group.
3. **Cognitive load was mischaracterized.** The two cited books distinguish intrinsic and extraneous loads; learning-relevant resources are not a third independent additive load. What is extraneous depends on the learner and learning goal. Brainstorming, syntax, and grammar can themselves be essential practice.
4. **Difficulty needs a purpose.** No cited source establishes a mandatory 15–20-minute struggle interval. Prior knowledge, achievable effort, feedback, and appropriate guidance matter. Worked examples can build understanding.
5. **Promising designs are not established effects.** The Cognitive Mirror is a proposal with a classroom illustration; Xiao measured prompt quality; Denny reported classroom use/perceptions; Smith studied grading. Kazemitabaar did not detect significant learning-outcome advantages for the engagement designs tested.
6. **Correlation and self-report were overstated.** Hong tested a bundled, nonrandomized program. Iqbal's “academic achievement” was self-reported sustainable/inclusive-education competence, not measured grades or independent performance.
7. **Effect sizes need their outcomes.** The reported `.55`, `.48`, and `.73` values were accurately transcribed. They describe self-explanation, mathematics worked examples, and self-assessment effects on self-efficacy respectively—not effect sizes for the blog's AI prompts.
8. **Two unsuitable review citations were removed from the post.** Gkintoni and Dong do not establish rapid, durable GenAI learning through selective offloading. Internal selection/reporting problems further weaken that use. Their bibliography entries remain because other content may cite them.
9. **Practical examples now preserve the proposed activity.** Learners compare alternatives before asking for a comparison, test generated explanations, and revisit tasks independently. The pointer example explicitly assumes a four-byte `int`; recursive string reversal includes call-stack space.
10. **The embedded quiz was aligned.** It no longer teaches universal skill amplification, and its offloading question now states a learning goal. The unsupported unreferenced quotation and the label “Requirement-Oriented Prompt Engineering (ROPE)” were removed; Denny's actual activity is “Prompt Problems.”

## Source-by-source evidence ledger

Page numbers below are **supplied PDF pages** unless explicitly labeled printed/journal/manuscript pages. A citation key is retained even where its spelling/year is an old internal identifier.

| Citation key | Evidence and correction |
|---|---|
| `BarbieriEtAl2023WorkedExamples` | Full substantive article, pp. 1–28. Meta-analysis: 43 articles, 55 studies, 181 effects; mathematics, not programming. Overall `g = .48`, 95% CI [.36, .60]. Too few fading studies to estimate fading separately (p. 25). Retained the effect with its domain and distinguished it from the proposed fading routine. |
| `BastaniEtAl2025` | Full eight-page supplied article; external supplementary information was not supplied. Almost 1,000 high-school students; relatively unrestricted GPT-4 improved practice but reduced subsequent unaided exam performance. Tutor safeguards largely mitigated harm. The authors explicitly limit inference to short-term outcomes (p. 2 footnote; p. 7 limitations). Removed long-term/neural-pathway explanations and guaranteed mitigation by any chosen usage style. |
| `BisraEtAl2018SelfExplanation` | Complete article. 64 reports, 69 effects, 5,917 participants; `g = .552`, 95% CI [.454, .650] (journal pp. 710–711). Prompts were researcher/instructor written; automated prompting was future work (p. 720). Preserved the effect and labeled chatbot routines as adaptations. |
| `Cepeda2006` | Main article and substantive appendix read. 317 experiments, 184 articles, 839 assessments of verbal recall. Benefits depend on both practice spacing and retention interval (journal pp. 365–371). Supports distributed practice, not a universal schedule or a chatbot's ability to remember and schedule retests. |
| `ChiWylie2014ICAP` | Complete substantive article, including tables and limitations. Constructive activity adds inferences beyond supplied information; interaction requires substantive reciprocal contributions (journal pp. 222–224). These are conditional predictions, not guarantees from talking to a chatbot. Teach-back description now reflects this. |
| `DORA2025` | Full 142-page report read, including methodology and notes. “Amplifier” is an organizational interpretation. Page 48 note 20 explicitly limits causal assurances from observational comparisons. Removed its use as proof of a universal novice/expert productivity or learning law. |
| `Dohmke2025` | Full supplied HTML essay, including linked-evidence context within the document. A practitioner argument about developer roles and retained judgment, not an independent controlled novice/expert experiment. Corrected missing coauthor Eirini Kalliamvakou and attributed the recommendation as an essay. |
| `Dong2026` | Complete substantive review pp. 1–19; reference metadata inspected. Reports positive pooled outcomes with very high heterogeneity and publication-bias signals. Table 2 includes AI-vs-student exam performance and remote orthopedic advice. Table 3 labels 74 university “studies” despite 53 included studies; forest plots include repeated outcomes without clear dependence handling. Its own reference list shows augmented-reality/Classkick sources used for GenAI assertions. Removed as support for accelerated durable learning; these concerns do not establish fabrication. |
| `Duplice2025` | Complete article and appendices. Classroom vocabulary study: 38 initial, 23 complete cases, three- and fifteen-week follow-ups. Generation showed a condition-by-time interaction, but no significant pairwise advantage at either follow-up; the author reports no clear overall benefit. No AI or neural-pathway measure. Moved citation to the conditionality of generation benefits. |
| `Ericson2017` | Entire article. 135 immediate-analysis participants, 82 complete delayed cases. Parsons practice faster than fixing/writing code, without detected outcome differences immediately or one week later; no self-reported load difference (`p = .30`, pp. 7–9). Corrected load claims, acknowledged syntax/indentation, and corrected coauthors. |
| `FanEtAl2025` | Entire article and appendices. AI-supported essay revision improved more, without a detectable between-condition advantage in knowledge gain or transfer; this does not mean the AI group learned nothing. “Metacognitive laziness” is an interpretation of process patterns, not a validated diagnosis or demonstrated causal mediation. Corrected both occurrences. |
| `Garcia2025` | Full substantive rapid review, pp. 1–20. 107 documents with diverse evidence and many conference papers; no pooled experiment demonstrating that requesting alternatives prevents fixation. Retained as a source of activity ideas, with learner-led comparison. Corrected Manuel B. Garcia and DOI. |
| `HongEtAl2025` | Entire ten-page article. 240 first-year English majors, four colleges, twelve weeks, intact nonrandom groups. AI brainstorming/outlining, critique, peer revision, and journals changed together (pp. 4–6). Greater critical-thinking/writing gains support the package, not an isolated offloading mechanism or inevitable reallocation of mental energy. |
| `Huang2025` | Full supplied v2 including appendices. Observations of 13 and survey of 99 experienced developers; no novice comparison and no learning outcome. Describes control and verification, not tutor-persona achievement gains. Removed mismatched tutoring citation; corrected Avery Reyna's name and recorded the version. |
| `IqbalEtAl2025` | All fifteen pages. Survey of 465 preservice teachers; variables self-reported. “Academic achievement” items concern sustainable/inclusive teaching competence (pp. 6–8), not grades. Page 13 explicitly disallows causal inference. Replaced a causal achievement claim with a qualified association. |
| `Kalyuga2003` | Complete article. Narrative review of expertise-by-instruction interactions (journal pp. 23–31); some effects disappear rather than reverse. Supports adapting/fading appropriate guidance, not heavy AI use per se. |
| `KalyugaPlass2025` | Entire supplied book: introduction, all seven chapters, references and index. Revised dual-load account, goal- and knowledge-dependent instruction; mixed findings for problem-first vs instruction-first (printed pp. 64–85). Corrected load categories and universal attempt-first/AI-heavy guidance claims. Fixed book DOI, previously the introduction chapter DOI. |
| `Kazemitabaar2025` | Complete substantive paper pp. 1–16, including both studies. Study 1: 82 participants/eight designs; Study 2: 42/three designs. No significant learning-outcome advantage; Study 1 Lead-and-Reveal `p = .058` before correction, Study 2 `p = .150`. Pregenerated correct code limits generalization. Retained as a design example, not proof that a tutor persona improves achievement or critical thinking. |
| `Kosmyna2025` | Full 216-page manuscript, including detailed results and appendices. 54 participants in initial sessions; 18 in crossover. Detailed reassigned-group quotation results pp. 46–48 contradict the directional account on p. 150. Figures/text checked visually. Removed the claim of a reliable recall benefit from “brain first”; electrical connectivity differences do not establish durable loss or brain damage. |
| `LodgeLoble2026` | Entire main report pp. 1–31 and annotated bibliography pp. 37–45; reference list used for identification. K–12 policy synthesis, not a new trial. Printed p. 18 rejects separate germane load; p. 27 discusses widening gaps as a risk. Removed its use as inevitable longitudinal evidence for professional developer skill divergence. |
| `Ma2026` | Complete manuscript and appendices. 36 graduate students; experience is self-reported, median-split. Technical experience predicts AI-assisted homework grades, but all students used AI, so this is not differential causal benefit. A diagnostic task's experience gap without AI was absent with AI. Retained evidence for useful behaviors, removed universal amplification and expert-professional characterization. |
| `PanaderoJonssonBotella2017SelfAssessment` | Entire substantive manuscript, appended tables and figure captions. 19 studies/2,305 learners. Learning self-regulation `d = .23`, CI [.08, .39]; two other self-regulation intervals include zero. Self-efficacy `d = .73`, CI [.39, 1.08], with possible inflation. Corrected outcome/uncertainty and separated the AI rubric routine from studied interventions. |
| `Paradis2025` | Full supplied twelve-page preprint; final publication metadata retained. Overall speed benefit; seniority-by-AI interaction `p = .706`, coding-hours interaction also nonsignificant. The draft's “significantly larger” boost for stronger foundations/system design confuses main effects with moderation. Corrected directly. |
| `Prather2024WideningGap` | Entire paper. 21 CS1 novices, all with AI available; qualitative observations/interviews, no expert sample or randomized no-AI comparator. Explicitly does not measure learning outcomes (p. 16). Preserved observed verification/overconfidence patterns without inferring causal learning-gap growth. |
| `RiskoGilbert2016` | Full substantive manuscript and all three boxes, glossary/captions. Offloading includes actions on the body/environment as well as tools. Manuscript p. 14 requires both irrelevant-to-learning demands being offloaded and saved resources being productively reallocated. Replaced “depends entirely on what” with goal, knowledge, delegation, and subsequent activity. |
| `RoedigerKarpicke2006` | Entire seven-page article, both experiments. Retrieval improved delayed prose recall relative to restudy; restudy could improve the immediate test (journal pp. 251–253). Retained delayed-retention rationale and separated retrieval from new inference/code tracing. Corrected author suffix. |
| `ShenTamkin2026` | Full manuscript and textual appendices. Main study: 52 developers with Python experience, unfamiliar with Trio; immediate assessment, no delayed retention. Usage styles were post-hoc and tiny (Generation-Then-Comprehension n=2; Hybrid n=3), not randomized interventions. Removed “high-performing students,” “completely bypass,” and causal full-mitigation claims. |
| `Slamecka1978` | Entire thirteen-page paper, all five experiments. Generating familiar words from constrained cues improved memory over reading. Not a test of failed programming attempts, neural priming, AI use, or a waiting period. Kept the specific result and marked the broader application. |
| `Smith2024ExplainPurpose` | Complete substantive paper pp. 1–6. Automated grading of 6,380 archived responses to 42 EiPE questions; moderate agreement, lenience toward line-by-line answers. Supports description of the assessment, not instructional efficacy or proof of complete understanding. Corrected authors and ITiCSE venue. |
| `Vorobyeva2025PersonalizedLearningThroughAI` | Complete substantive review pp. 1–18, all tables. Qualitative review of thirty heterogeneous papers. Supports possibilities for personalized feedback/adaptation; flags accuracy, validation, small samples and short interventions (pp. 14, 17–18). Filled the previously empty feedback bullet with a bounded recommendation. |
| `Yan2024promises` | Complete substantive Perspective pp. 1–8, boxes/tables/figure captions; references inspected. Possibilities and risks, not a new trial. Explicit evidence limitations and need for longitudinal/randomized work (p. 3); expert validation and methodological rigor (pp. 4, 8). Retained as qualified framing. |
| `bjork2011making` | Entire supplied chapter. Desirable difficulty requires prerequisites and capacity to respond (printed pp. 58, 61–62). No mandatory fifteen-minute protocol. Replaced universal challenge/struggle rule with appropriate attempts and help. |
| `bjork2020desirable` | Entire supplied manuscript. Distinguishes performance from learning and productive from excessive difficulty (manuscript pp. 2–3, 6). No universal waiting period; not all questions instantiate retrieval. Corrected accordingly. |
| `brown2014MakeItStick` | Preface, all eight chapters (printed pp. 1–253), and all endnotes (pp. 257–284). Difficulty must fit knowledge and achievable effort (pp. 98–99); generation need not be exceptionally challenging (p. 260 note 7). The “retrieval route” account is one explanation (p. 88), not a measured AI neural mechanism. Retained practical principles; corrected Roediger suffix. |
| `deBruinEtAl2023` | Complete substantive framework paper, pp. 1–22. Motivation, perceived effort, and sustained strategy use; no AI neural-pathway trial. Notes uncertainty about effort calibration and time-management prescriptions. Moved citation to the conditional nature of productive difficulty. |
| `denny2024prompt` | Entire substantive paper pp. 1–6. “Prompt Problems,” 15-person pilot and optional classroom use; task performance/perceptions, not controlled learning gains. No named ROPE framework. Replaced label and activity description; added missing Becker and Reeves coauthors. |
| `gkintonl2025` | Full substantive review pp. 1–91, every included-study table row; references used for identification. Primarily broad AI/neuroadaptive research. Table includes vehicle simulations (p. 27), mice and chemotherapy/fMRI studies despite educational scope; PRISMA arithmetic inconsistent (p. 10). Prediction accuracy becomes a learning-gain percentage (pp. 51, 67), and some result summaries contradict table entries. Key pages visually verified. Removed from broad GenAI/offloading efficacy claims without alleging fabrication. |
| `kestin2025` | Complete ten-page supplied article; external supplementary material was not supplied. Designed physics tutor, randomized classroom comparison; more than double median pre-to-post gain, median AI time 49 minutes versus estimated 60 minutes of learning within a 75-minute class. The gain comparison uses group posttest medians and a pooled pretest median. Immediate outcome from this design, not generic-chatbot or long-term evidence. Corrected Gregorio Ponti, full title and DOI. |
| `pardos2024` | Complete eighteen-page supplied article, including its separate self-consistency analysis. 274 adults; screened worked solutions, not one-hint-at-a-time tutoring. AI vs human gain difference `p = .416`; not a formal equivalence test. Initial 32% quality failures excluded before learning trial; self-consistency evaluated separately. Clarified screening, uncertainty, and scope. |
| `sweller2011cognitive` | Preface and all eighteen chapters (printed pp. 3–242), tables/examples/qualifications. Two additive loads and learning-relevant resources (pp. 57–58, 68–69); worked examples and dynamic adaptation support novice learning. Corrected three-load and “complete examples only train recognition” claims. |
| `tomisu2025` | Entire seven-page Perspective. Proposed Cognitive Mirror and 36-student classroom illustration, without randomization/control/systematic outcome assessment (pp. 4–5). No causal efficacy claim justified. AI confusion or agreement is not an authoritative assessment of the learner. |
| `xiao2024` | Entire supplied 21-page manuscript and appendix. 36-teacher formative work and 22-student uncontrolled pre/post intervention. All six prompt components improved; programming skill/retention not assessed. Corrected citation from nonexistent 2024 Learning@Scale publication to 2025 arXiv preprint, preserving the key. |
| `yan2025distinguishing` | Entire five-page accepted manuscript. Commentary distinguishing performance from durable independent retention/transfer; mechanisms and long-term effects expressly remain research questions (pp. 3–4). Retained as framing, not independent causal confirmation of Fan's interpretation. |
